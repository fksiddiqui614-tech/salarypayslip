$60,000 a Year Is How Much an Hour?

A $60,000 annual salary is approximately $28.85 an hour when you work 40 hours per week for 52 weeks a year. See the complete monthly, weekly, biweekly, daily and hourly breakdown.

Quick Answer: $60,000 a Year Is $28.85 an Hour

If you earn $60,000 per year and work a standard 40-hour workweek for 52 weeks, your gross hourly rate is approximately $28.85 per hour.

Annual Salary $60,000 Gross yearly pay
Hourly $28.85 40 hrs/week
Monthly $5,000 Before deductions
Weekly $1,153.85 52 weeks
Biweekly $2,307.69 26 pay periods
Daily $230.77 8-hour day
Standard calculation: $60,000 ÷ (40 hours × 52 weeks) = $28.85 per hour. This is a gross salary calculation before taxes and other deductions.

A $60,000 salary can be converted into several useful pay periods. Assuming the salary is spread evenly across 52 weeks and you work 40 hours per week, the gross pay looks like this:

Pay Period Calculation Gross Pay

These figures are gross amounts. Actual paychecks can be lower after federal, state and local taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, and other payroll deductions.

How Does $60,000 a Year Become $28.85 an Hour?

The hourly equivalent of an annual salary depends on how many hours you work during the year. The standard full-time calculation uses 40 hours per week and 52 weeks per year.

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ (Hours per Week × Weeks per Year)
$60,000 ÷ (40 × 52)
$60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85/hour

There are 2,080 scheduled working hours in a standard 40-hour work year when calculated as 40 hours × 52 weeks. Dividing $60,000 by 2,080 gives approximately $28.85 per hour.

If your schedule is different from 40 hours per week, your effective hourly rate will change even if your annual salary remains $60,000.

What If You Work 50 or 48 Weeks a Year?

The standard $28.85 calculation assumes 52 weeks of work in the annual salary calculation. If you want to calculate an effective hourly rate based only on weeks actually worked, the result changes.

Working Schedule Annual Hours Effective Hourly Rate
40 hrs × 52 weeks 2,080 $28.85
40 hrs × 50 weeks 2,000 $30.00
40 hrs × 48 weeks 1,920 $31.25
Important: If your $60,000 salary is paid for the full year and your paid vacation is included in your compensation, the standard salary-equivalent hourly rate remains $28.85. The 50- and 48-week figures represent an effective rate when measuring only the weeks actually worked.

Your actual paycheck schedule may differ. For example, employers can pay weekly, biweekly, semimonthly or monthly. The annual salary remains $60,000 before deductions as long as the full salary is earned.

Is $28.85 an Hour Before or After Taxes?

The $28.85 hourly figure is gross pay, not take-home pay. It represents the salary before taxes and other payroll deductions.

Your actual take-home amount can be different depending on factors such as federal income taxes, state and local taxes, filing status, health insurance, retirement contributions and other deductions.

This means you should not assume that $28.85 is the amount that will appear in your bank account for every hour worked. It is the salary-equivalent hourly rate used to convert the $60,000 annual salary.

Gross vs. net: $28.85/hour is the gross equivalent of $60,000 per year under the standard 40-hour, 52-week schedule. Your net hourly amount will depend on your individual payroll situation.

Is $60,000 a Year a Good Salary?

Whether $60,000 is a good salary depends heavily on where you live, your household size, housing costs, taxes, debt, benefits and personal spending needs.

The same $60,000 salary can provide a very different standard of living in different cities and regions. Benefits such as health insurance, paid time off, retirement contributions and bonuses can also affect the overall value of a job.

If you are comparing a $60,000 salaried position with an hourly job, look beyond the headline salary and compare the actual hours worked, paid time off, benefits and expected deductions.

What Is $60,000 a Year at 50 Hours a Week?

If you work 50 hours every week for 52 weeks, you would work 2,600 hours during the year.

$60,000 ÷ 2,600 = $23.08 per hour

So while the standard salary-equivalent rate is $28.85 per hour at 40 hours per week, the effective rate falls to approximately $23.08 per hour if you regularly work 50 hours per week without an increase in annual salary.

This is one reason hourly-equivalent calculations can be useful when evaluating salaried employment offers.
