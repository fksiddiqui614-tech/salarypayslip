How Many Working Hours Are in a Year?

For a standard full-time schedule, there are 2,080 working hours in a year. This is calculated using a 40-hour workweek and 52 weeks:

40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 working hours

The 2,080 figure is a standard annual-hours benchmark used for salary conversions and other workplace calculations. It assumes the employee's schedule is 40 hours per week across 52 weeks.

However, 2,080 is a standard calculation rather than the exact number of hours every employee physically works. Holidays, vacation, PTO, sick leave, unpaid time off and different weekly schedules can reduce the actual hours worked during a particular year.

Where Does the 2,080-Hour Work Year Come From?

The calculation is straightforward. A traditional full-time schedule has 8 hours per day, 5 days per week, which equals 40 hours per week. Multiplying that weekly schedule by 52 weeks gives 2,080 hours.

8 hours × 5 days × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours

The same result can also be expressed as 260 standard workdays multiplied by 8 hours per day:

260 workdays × 8 hours = 2,080 hours

How Many Working Hours Are in 2026?

The standard salary calculation remains 2,080 hours, but the actual calendar for 2026 is slightly different.

2026 contains 261 weekdays. If a person works 8 hours on each weekday, that produces 2,088 weekday hours before holidays, vacation and other time off are removed.

2,080 vs. 2,088: 2,080 is the standard 40-hours × 52-weeks salary benchmark. The 2,088 figure comes from the actual number of Monday-to-Friday weekdays in the 2026 calendar before holidays and leave.

This distinction matters because a calendar year is not exactly 52 weeks: 52 weeks contain 364 days, while a normal calendar year contains 365 days. Leap years contain 366 days.

How Many Working Hours Are in a Month?

The standard 2,080-hour work year averages approximately 173.33 working hours per month.

2,080 ÷ 12 = 173.33 hours per month

This is an annual average rather than a statement that every month contains exactly 173.33 working hours. Actual monthly hours change depending on the calendar, holidays and the number of working days in each month.

How Many Working Hours Are in a Week?

For the standard full-time schedule used in this calculation, a workweek contains 40 hours.

That usually means 8 hours per day across 5 working days:

8 hours × 5 days = 40 hours per week

Some employees work different schedules, including four-day workweeks, part-time schedules, compressed schedules or longer workweeks. Their annual hours should be calculated from their actual weekly schedule.

Do 2,080 Working Hours Include Holidays and PTO?

No. The standard 2,080-hour calculation does not subtract holidays, vacation, PTO or sick leave. It simply multiplies 40 hours by 52 weeks.

For example, suppose an employee normally works 40 hours per week and takes 10 workdays away from work. At 8 hours per day, those 10 days represent 80 hours.

2,080 − 80 = 2,000 hours

The resulting number is an example of scheduled hours after removing those 10 days. Your actual number depends on your employer's holiday policy, vacation allowance and other time away from work.

Important: Paid time off and actual hours worked are not always the same thing. A salaried employee may still receive their full annual salary while taking paid vacation or paid holidays. The appropriate annual-hours figure depends on what you are trying to measure.

How Many Hours Do You Actually Work in a Year?

There is no single number that applies to every employee. Your actual annual hours depend on your weekly schedule and the amount of time you spend away from work.

For example, someone working 40 hours per week with two weeks of unpaid time off would have:

40 × 50 = 2,000 working hours

Someone working 37.5 hours per week for 52 weeks would have:

37.5 × 52 = 1,950 working hours

This is why an annual work-hours calculation should always start with the actual schedule being measured.

How Working Hours Affect Salary-to-Hourly Calculations

The number of annual working hours is especially important when converting an annual salary into an hourly equivalent.

For a standard full-time schedule, the formula is:

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ 2,080

For example, a $60,000 annual salary divided by 2,080 hours gives an hourly equivalent of approximately $28.85.

Likewise, a $100,000 annual salary divided by 2,080 hours equals approximately $48.08 per hour.

If your schedule is different from 40 hours per week, you should use your actual annual hours instead of automatically using 2,080.

Working Hours vs. Calendar Hours

A calendar year contains far more total hours than a person normally spends working. A standard year has 365 days, and each day contains 24 hours.

365 × 24 = 8,760 calendar hours

A leap year contains 366 days and therefore has:

366 × 24 = 8,784 calendar hours

These figures should not be confused with working hours. The 2,080-hour figure represents a standard full-time work schedule, not the total number of hours in the calendar year.

Why the 2,080-Hour Number Matters

The 2,080-hour benchmark is useful because it creates a consistent way to compare annual salaries and hourly pay.

For example:

  • $40,000 ÷ 2,080 ≈ $19.23 per hour
  • $50,000 ÷ 2,080 ≈ $24.04 per hour
  • $60,000 ÷ 2,080 ≈ $28.85 per hour
  • $75,000 ÷ 2,080 ≈ $36.06 per hour
  • $100,000 ÷ 2,080 ≈ $48.08 per hour

These are gross hourly equivalents before taxes and other deductions. If your annual schedule uses different hours or weeks, the result will change accordingly.
