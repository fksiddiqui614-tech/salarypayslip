How Do You Calculate Hourly Rate From Annual Salary?

To calculate an hourly rate from an annual salary, divide the annual salary by the total number of paid working hours in a year.

For a standard full-time schedule of 40 hours per week and 52 weeks per year, the calculation uses 2,080 annual hours.

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ (Hours Per Week × Weeks Per Year)

Because 40 × 52 equals 2,080, the standard shortcut is:

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ 2,080

This produces a gross hourly equivalent. It does not automatically account for taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, bonuses, overtime or other deductions.

Step-by-Step: Convert Annual Salary to Hourly Pay

Step 1: Find Your Annual Salary

Start with your gross annual salary before taxes and payroll deductions. For example, suppose your salary is $60,000 per year.

Step 2: Determine Your Weekly Hours

Next, determine how many hours you normally work each week. A standard full-time schedule is often 40 hours per week.

Step 3: Determine Your Paid Weeks Per Year

For the standard calculation, use 52 weeks per year. If you have unpaid weeks away from work, use the number of weeks for which the annual salary actually covers your working schedule.

Step 4: Calculate Annual Working Hours

40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours

Step 5: Divide Salary by Annual Hours

Now divide the annual salary by the annual working hours:

$60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85 per hour

So, using the standard 40-hour, 52-week schedule, a $60,000 salary has an hourly equivalent of approximately $28.85 per hour.

Annual Salary to Hourly Rate Formula

The general formula works for any annual salary and schedule:

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ (Hours Per Week × Weeks Per Year)

For example, if you earn $75,000 and work 40 hours per week for 52 weeks:

$75,000 ÷ (40 × 52) = $75,000 ÷ 2,080 = $36.06/hour

The same formula can be used for salaries of $50,000, $80,000, $100,000 or any other annual amount.

For example, if someone earns $60,000 but works 35 hours per week, the calculation is:

$60,000 ÷ (35 × 52) = $32.97/hour

This is why using 2,080 automatically is not appropriate for every work arrangement. The denominator should match the schedule you are trying to measure.

How to Calculate Hourly Rate With Unpaid Time Off

If you receive an annual salary but have unpaid weeks away from work, you can adjust the calculation by using the actual paid weeks.

For example, assume a $60,000 salary covers 50 paid working weeks at 40 hours per week:

40 × 50 = 2,000 annual hours

$60,000 ÷ 2,000 = $30.00/hour

The standard 2,080-hour calculation would produce $28.85, while the effective rate based on 2,000 paid working hours would be $30.00.

Important: Do not automatically subtract paid vacation or paid holidays from the standard salary calculation. If you receive your normal salary during those periods, the standard annual salary equivalent can still use the agreed paid schedule. Adjust the annual hours when you are specifically measuring actual hours worked or unpaid time.

How to Calculate Hourly Rate From Monthly Salary

If you only know your monthly salary, first convert it into an annual salary by multiplying the monthly amount by 12.

Annual Salary = Monthly Salary × 12

For example, if your monthly salary is $5,000:

$5,000 × 12 = $60,000 annual salary

$60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85/hour

This method is useful when comparing a monthly compensation offer with an hourly position.

How to Convert Hourly Pay Back to Annual Salary

The reverse calculation is just as simple. Multiply your hourly rate by the number of annual working hours.

Annual Salary = Hourly Rate × Annual Working Hours

Using the standard 2,080-hour schedule, someone earning $25 per hour would have an annualized salary of:

$25 × 2,080 = $52,000 per year

This is an annualized gross figure based on the assumed schedule. Actual earnings can differ if the employee works fewer or more hours, takes unpaid time off or receives overtime.

Gross Hourly Rate vs. Take-Home Hourly Pay

The hourly rate calculated from annual salary is normally a gross hourly equivalent.

For example, if your annual salary is $60,000, the standard calculation gives approximately $28.85 per hour. That does not mean $28.85 is the amount deposited into your bank account for every hour worked.

Federal taxes, state or local taxes, Social Security, Medicare, health insurance, retirement contributions and other deductions can reduce take-home pay.

Because deductions vary from person to person, a salary-to-hourly calculation should not be confused with an after-tax hourly rate.

Why Calculate Your Hourly Rate?

Converting a salary into an hourly equivalent can make compensation easier to compare.

  • Compare a salaried job with an hourly job.
  • Evaluate two job offers with different schedules.
  • Understand the value of longer working hours.
  • Estimate the effect of unpaid time off.
  • Compare annual salary with freelance or contract rates.
  • Convert between annual and hourly compensation.

For example, two jobs may both offer $70,000 per year, but one could require 40 hours per week while another regularly requires substantially more time. The annual salary alone does not show that difference.

Common Mistakes When Converting Salary to Hourly

Using 2,080 for Every Schedule

2,080 is a standard benchmark, not a universal divisor. If you work 35 hours per week, your annual scheduled hours are 1,820 rather than 2,080.

Ignoring Unpaid Weeks

If your salary does not cover the entire year, use the appropriate number of paid weeks when measuring the effective hourly rate.

Confusing Gross and Net Pay

Annual salary normally refers to gross compensation. Taxes and deductions should not be removed before performing the basic salary-to-hourly calculation.

Forgetting Actual Working Hours

If you regularly work overtime or additional unpaid hours, your effective hourly value based on actual time worked can be lower than the standard salary equivalent.
