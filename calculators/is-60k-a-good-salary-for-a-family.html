Is $60K a Good Salary for a Family in 2026?

For a family, $60,000 a year is generally considered workable and closer to comfortable than $50,000 — the extra $10,000 meaningfully eases the pressure from childcare and health insurance costs, though larger families in expensive metro areas will still feel the squeeze.

💡

Quick answer: $60,000 supports a small-to-medium family reasonably well in low-to-average cost-of-living areas, and is workable (though tight) even in some higher cost areas — a clear step up from the tighter $50,000 scenario.

The Family Budget Gap at $60,000

Childcare and insurance costs don't scale down with a higher parent salary — they stay roughly the same in dollar terms, which means a higher income directly translates to a smaller percentage bite.

Tax Credits That Help Families at $60,000

  • Child Tax Credit (CTC): up to $2,000 per qualifying child. (Source: IRS.gov)
  • Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC): phases out at higher incomes, so a $60,000 household — especially with multiple earners or fewer children — may qualify for less EITC than a $50,000 household. (Source: IRS.gov)

This is one area where $60,000 doesn't uniformly outperform $50,000 — EITC eligibility can actually shrink as income rises, which is worth factoring into the full picture.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a family of 4 live on $60,000 a year?+

Yes, a family of 4 can live reasonably well on $60,000 a year in low-to-average cost-of-living areas, and workably even in some higher cost areas — a clear step up from a $50,000 budget.

How much does childcare cost relative to a $60,000 family income?+

Full-time childcare for one child typically costs $800 to $1,500 a month, which takes up roughly 18-33% of take-home pay on a $60,000 salary — noticeably less strain than on a $50,000 salary.

Is $60,000 enough for a single-income family?+

A single income of $60,000 is workable for a family in most low-to-average cost-of-living areas, though still tight in expensive metro areas like New York or Los Angeles.

Does EITC eligibility change between $50,000 and $60,000?+

Yes, the Earned Income Tax Credit phases out at higher income levels, so a family earning $60,000 may qualify for a smaller credit than one earning $50,000, depending on filing status and number of children.
