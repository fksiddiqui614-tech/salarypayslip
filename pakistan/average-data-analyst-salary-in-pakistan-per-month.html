Data Analyst Salary in Pakistan: Quick Answer

A data analyst in Pakistan earns PKR 80,000 to PKR 280,000 per month in most local roles. The job is centred on Excel, SQL and a BI tool (Power BI or Tableau) to turn existing company data into reports and dashboards for managers. It is one of the more accessible entry points into a tech career because it does not require a computer science degree or heavy programming, which is also why entry-level pay is lower than for developer or data science roles.

Which Tool Actually Raises a Data Analyst's Pay

Not every tool on a CV moves the number the same amount. Roughly, in order of impact:

SkillEffect on pay
SQL (writing your own queries, not just running saved ones)Largest single jump — moves a candidate out of pure-Excel roles
Power BI or Tableau (building dashboards, not just viewing them)Second largest — most Pakistani employers now expect this by year 2-3
Python or R (pandas, basic automation)Moderate — matters more for roles that overlap with data science
Advanced Excel (Power Query, PivotTables, macros)Useful floor skill, but on its own caps pay at the entry level
Statistics (correlation, basic hypothesis testing)Matters most for analysts trying to move toward data science

A data analyst who can only build things a manager could technically build themselves in Excel gets paid like a report-writer. One who can pull and clean data independently with SQL, and present it through a dashboard the business actually relies on, gets paid like a technical hire.

Who Actually Becomes a Data Analyst in Pakistan

Data analyst is one of the few tech-adjacent roles that regularly hires people without a computer science background. Common entry paths include finance or commerce graduates who are strong in Excel, business or economics graduates who learn SQL and Power BI on the side, and career switchers from accounting or operations roles who already understand the business data they are being asked to report on. This is different from a data scientist or software engineer role, where a technical degree or strong self-taught coding portfolio is close to a requirement.

Remote and Freelance Data Analyst Work

Remote data analyst roles exist but are less common than remote developer or data science roles, because a lot of analyst work depends on understanding a specific company's internal data and systems. Where remote work does happen, it is usually reporting, dashboard-building or ad hoc analysis for an overseas company's existing data, paid by the hour or on a fixed monthly retainer, and it pays noticeably more than the same work done locally.

How to Start as a Data Analyst in Pakistan

  1. Get comfortable with Excel beyond basic formulas: PivotTables, Power Query, VLOOKUP/XLOOKUP.
  2. Learn SQL well enough to write your own queries against a real database, not just copy examples.
  3. Learn one BI tool properly — Power BI is more commonly requested locally than Tableau.
  4. Build 2-3 small projects with public datasets and turn them into dashboards, not just charts.
  5. Apply for junior analyst, reporting analyst or business analyst roles — the titles are used loosely, so read the actual responsibilities, not just the title.

Of the tech-adjacent roles on this site, data analyst is one of the least portable across borders, for a reason that has nothing to do with skill: much of the job is understanding one specific employer's internal data, systems and business context, which does not transfer the way a coding portfolio does. This is also why remote analyst work (covered above) tends to be project-based reporting or dashboard work rather than the ongoing full-time remote roles common for developers — a foreign company can more easily hire a remote developer to build a feature than a remote analyst to understand data they cannot easily show an outsider.

Where international movement does happen, it is usually a step up in title as well as pay: a Pakistani "Data Analyst" moving to the UAE, UK or North America often moves into a "Business Intelligence Analyst" or "Business Analyst" role, since those markets separate the reporting-focused work described on this page from the deeper technical analytics work covered in the Data Scientist guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average data analyst salary in Pakistan per month?

About PKR 80,000 to PKR 280,000 per month for local roles, higher with strong SQL and BI-tool skills.

How much does a junior data analyst earn in Pakistan?

Junior data analysts usually earn PKR 45,000 to PKR 80,000 per month, doing mostly Excel and basic SQL reporting.

Which skill raises a data analyst's salary the most?

Moving from Excel-only reporting to writing your own SQL queries usually causes the biggest single jump in pay, followed by building dashboards in Power BI or Tableau.

Do I need a computer science degree to become a data analyst?

No. Many data analysts in Pakistan come from finance, commerce, economics or business backgrounds and learn SQL and BI tools separately.

Can data analysts work remotely?

Yes, but less commonly than developer or data science roles, since analyst work often depends on a specific employer's internal data and systems.

Which industry pays data analysts the most in Pakistan?

Banking and finance generally pay the most, partly because of regulatory reporting requirements that raise the bar for accuracy.

Can a data analyst become a data scientist?

Yes, but it typically takes one to two years of learning statistics, Python and machine learning on top of existing analyst skills.

Which city offers the best data analyst opportunities in Pakistan?

Karachi, followed by Lahore and Islamabad, because of the concentration of banks, telecom companies and multinational offices.

Is data analytics a good entry point into a tech career?

Yes. It is one of the more accessible tech-adjacent roles because it does not require heavy programming or a technical degree to get started.

Conclusion

A data analyst in Pakistan typically earns PKR 80,000 to PKR 280,000 per month, with the biggest pay jumps coming from SQL and BI-tool skills rather than from experience alone. It is one of the more accessible ways into a tech career since it does not require a computer science degree, and from here the clearest paths forward are toward BI development, business analysis, or — for those willing to learn statistics and Python — data science.
