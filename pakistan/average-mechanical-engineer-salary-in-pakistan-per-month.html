Mechanical Engineer Salary in Pakistan: Quick Answer

A mechanical engineer in Pakistan earns PKR 70,000 to PKR 550,000 per month in most roles, with engineering managers and consultants going higher. Unlike a tech role where a portfolio can move pay quickly, mechanical engineering pay is anchored by two things: which industry you work in, and whether you're registered with the Pakistan Engineering Council (PEC) — both matter more here than in most careers on this site.

PEC Registration and Why It Matters for Pay

Mechanical engineering is a regulated profession in Pakistan. Registration with the Pakistan Engineering Council (PEC) — first as a Graduate Engineer, then as a Registered Professional Engineer after enough supervised experience — is required or strongly preferred for most engineering roles, and is a hard requirement for signing off on many government and infrastructure projects.

An unregistered engineer can still work, especially early in a manufacturing or maintenance role, but the jump into project engineer, lead engineer or independent consulting roles is much harder without PEC registration, since these roles often legally require a registered engineer to sign off on the work. This is why the qualification table below shows a real pay jump at "Registered Professional Engineer" rather than just at a degree level.

Unlike IT certifications, PEC registration doesn't automatically transfer abroad — each destination has its own licensing pathway. The Gulf states are the most direct route: most employers there hire on the strength of your PEC registration and experience, with employer sponsorship rather than a separate exam. Canada, Australia and the UK require a formal skills assessment or provincial licensing process that can take a year or more, which is why the Gulf remains the first move for most Pakistani mechanical engineers going abroad, and Canada/UK/Australia are typically a second move once a candidate already has strong international project experience.

How to Build a Mechanical Engineering Career in Pakistan

  1. Complete a PEC-accredited BS in Mechanical Engineering.
  2. Register with PEC as a Graduate Engineer immediately after graduating — this starts the clock toward full registration.
  3. Get trainee or junior experience, ideally in a higher-paying sector (oil & gas, power) if you can, since early sector choice compounds over a career.
  4. Apply for full PEC registration as a Professional Engineer once you have the required supervised experience.
  5. Add one high-value software skill (ANSYS or CATIA) once you know which specialization you're heading toward.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average mechanical engineer salary in Pakistan per month?

About PKR 70,000 to PKR 550,000 per month, depending mainly on industry sector and PEC registration status, more than years of experience alone.

How much does a fresh mechanical engineer earn in Pakistan?

Fresh graduates typically start as trainee engineers earning PKR 70,000 to PKR 110,000 per month.

Do government mechanical engineers earn more than private ones?

Not usually. Government roles offer structured scales, pension and stability, while private industry — especially oil & gas and power — generally pays more for the same experience level.

Which industry pays mechanical engineers the most in Pakistan?

Oil & gas and power generation pay the most, up to PKR 600,000–700,000+ for senior roles, because of the scale and risk of the projects involved.

Does PEC registration affect salary?

Yes, significantly. Full PEC registration as a Professional Engineer is often required to sign off on projects independently, which is why registered engineers typically earn PKR 180,000–350,000+ versus a much lower range for unregistered graduates.

Which city offers the best mechanical engineering salaries?

Karachi generally pays the most, due to its concentration of shipyards, refineries and heavy industry, followed by Lahore and Islamabad.

Can mechanical engineers work abroad?

Yes. The Gulf states are the most direct route, since employers there typically hire on PEC registration and experience with employer sponsorship. The UK, Canada and Australia require a separate skills assessment or licensing process.

Is mechanical engineering still a good career in Pakistan?

Yes, particularly for engineers who target higher-paying sectors early (oil & gas, power) and complete PEC registration, since both have a larger effect on lifetime earnings than the degree alone.

Conclusion

A mechanical engineer in Pakistan typically earns PKR 70,000 to PKR 550,000 per month, with engineering managers and consultants going higher. The two decisions that matter most for lifetime earnings are which industry you enter — oil & gas and power pay considerably more than textiles or general manufacturing — and completing PEC registration, which most senior and project-lead roles require. Software skills like ANSYS or CATIA and international project experience are the next-strongest levers once those two are in place.
