What Does an ERP Consultant Do in Pakistan?

An ERP Consultant helps an organisation use enterprise resource planning software to manage its business operations more efficiently. Instead of simply installing software, the consultant studies how a company currently handles finance, sales, purchasing, inventory, human resources, manufacturing or other processes and then maps those requirements into an ERP system.

The role sits between business operations and technology. A consultant may speak with department managers to understand their requirements, configure ERP modules, test workflows, identify problems, prepare documentation and train employees before and after an implementation.

The exact job can look very different depending on the employer. A junior consultant may spend most of the day supporting users and preparing configuration documents, while an experienced consultant may lead implementation projects, manage client requirements, design workflows and coordinate developers, analysts and business teams.

💡
Why ERP Consulting Is Different

ERP consulting is not purely a programming career and it is not purely a business role. The strongest consultants understand both sides: they know how a business process works and how an ERP platform can represent, automate or improve that process.

ERP Implementation Lifecycle: What Consultants Actually Handle

One of the clearest ways to understand ERP consulting is to follow a project from its first client meeting to the point where employees begin using the new system. The consultant's responsibilities can change at every stage of this process.

1. Business Discovery

The consultant meets business teams and documents how existing processes work. This can include finance, purchasing, sales, inventory, production or reporting workflows.

2. Requirement Analysis

The consultant identifies what the organisation actually needs from the ERP system and separates essential requirements from processes that may be simplified.

3. Configuration

ERP modules are configured according to the approved business requirements. Depending on the platform, this may involve workflows, permissions, forms, reports, tax settings and other system parameters.

4. Testing

Consultants test business scenarios before deployment. They check whether transactions, approvals, reports and integrations behave as expected and document issues that need correction.

5. Training & Preparation

Users need to understand how their daily work will change. Consultants may prepare training material, conduct demonstrations and help departments practise important workflows.

6. Go-Live & Support

After deployment, consultants help resolve early operational problems, answer user questions, monitor workflows and make adjustments as the organisation begins working with the new system.

⚙️
Why Implementation Experience Matters for Salary

A consultant who has participated in complete implementations can demonstrate a broader skill set than someone whose experience is limited to basic ERP support. End-to-end project exposure can therefore become an important career accelerator.

Functional vs Technical ERP Consultant: What Is the Difference?

ERP consulting is commonly divided into functional, technical and techno-functional paths. Understanding the difference is useful when choosing a career direction because each path requires a different combination of business and technology skills.

Consulting Path Main Focus Important Skills
Functional ERP Consultant Business processes and ERP module configuration Requirement gathering, process mapping, ERP modules, communication and user training
Technical ERP Consultant Customisation, integrations and technical system requirements Programming, databases, APIs, system architecture and troubleshooting
Techno-Functional Consultant Connecting business requirements with technical solutions ERP configuration, business analysis, integrations, SQL and technical communication

A finance graduate who understands accounting workflows may naturally move toward functional ERP consulting, while a computer science graduate with database and programming knowledge may prefer technical consulting. Professionals who develop both skill sets can eventually move into techno-functional, solution architecture or implementation leadership roles.

Important ERP Modules for Consultants

ERP systems are divided into modules that represent different areas of an organisation. Consultants normally specialise in one or more modules rather than becoming equally advanced in every part of the platform.

💰 Finance & Accounting

Includes general ledger, accounts payable, accounts receivable, budgeting, financial reporting and transaction controls. Finance knowledge is particularly valuable for consultants working with accounting teams.

📦 Inventory & Supply Chain

Covers purchasing, stock management, warehouses, suppliers, logistics and product movement. Consultants in this area need to understand how physical inventory connects with ERP transactions.

🛒 Sales & CRM

Sales modules can manage leads, quotations, orders, customers, pricing and invoicing. Consultants help translate sales processes into consistent system workflows.

👥 Human Resources

HR-related ERP functions may include employee records, attendance, leave, payroll and organisational information. Knowledge of HR operations can support specialised consulting roles.

🏭 Manufacturing

Manufacturing ERP can cover bills of materials, production orders, planning, quality and material requirements. Consultants need strong knowledge of production workflows.

📊 Reporting & Business Intelligence

ERP reporting converts operational transactions into information for managers. Consultants may work with dashboards, reports, data structures and business performance indicators.

How to Become an ERP Consultant in Pakistan

ERP consulting is accessible through several educational backgrounds because companies need professionals who understand both software and business operations. A graduate can enter through an ERP support, implementation executive, business analyst or junior consultant position and gradually develop deeper platform expertise.

Step 1: Build a Relevant Foundation

A degree in computer science, information technology, business, accounting, finance, supply chain or a related discipline can provide a useful starting point.

Step 2: Learn One ERP Platform

Choose a platform and learn its major modules, terminology, configuration concepts and typical business workflows instead of trying to master several systems simultaneously.

Step 3: Understand Business Processes

Learn how purchasing, sales, inventory, accounting, payroll, manufacturing and reporting work in real organisations.

Step 4: Practise Implementation

Create sample companies, configure workflows, import test data and document implementation scenarios to develop practical experience.

Step 5: Start in Support or Junior Consulting

Entry-level ERP support and implementation positions can provide exposure to real users, system issues, testing and configuration.

Step 6: Move Into Implementation

After gaining experience, take responsibility for requirements, modules, client communication, testing and complete implementation phases.

🚀
The Most Valuable Early Experience

Try to participate in real implementation work rather than limiting your experience to ERP data entry or basic technical support. Exposure to requirements, configuration, testing and user training creates a much stronger foundation for long-term consulting work.

Frequently Asked Questions About ERP Consultant Salary in Pakistan

What is the average ERP Consultant salary in Pakistan?

ERP Consultant salaries vary considerably by experience, ERP platform and job type. A practical market benchmark is around PKR 45,000–120,000 per month, while current salary reporting places the national median total pay near PKR 60,000 per month.

What is the starting salary of an ERP Consultant in Pakistan?

Entry-level ERP professionals can typically expect around PKR 35,000–50,000 per month. Candidates with relevant ERP training, business-process knowledge or technical skills may qualify for stronger starting offers.

How much can an ERP Consultant earn after 3 years?

After around three years of relevant experience, an ERP Consultant may reach approximately PKR 70,000–120,000 per month, particularly when they can independently handle requirements, configuration, testing and implementation tasks.

Can an ERP Consultant earn PKR 200,000 per month in Pakistan?

Yes. Salaries around PKR 200,000 or more are possible for experienced consultants, ERP specialists, implementation leads and professionals working with complex enterprise projects or international clients. They are not typical entry-level salaries.

Is ERP consulting a good career in Pakistan?

ERP consulting can be a strong career for people who enjoy combining technology with business operations. It provides career paths into functional consulting, technical consulting, solution architecture, project management and enterprise applications leadership.

Do ERP Consultants need to know programming?

Not always. Functional consultants can work mainly with business processes, requirements and ERP configuration. Technical and techno-functional consultants benefit more from programming, SQL, databases, APIs and integration knowledge.

What degree is best for becoming an ERP Consultant?

Computer science, information technology, business administration, management information systems, accounting, finance and supply chain degrees can all provide useful foundations. The best choice depends on the ERP specialisation a candidate wants to pursue.

Is SAP better than Odoo for an ERP Consultant career?

Neither platform is universally better. SAP is strongly associated with large enterprise environments, while Odoo is widely used by smaller and growing organisations. The better choice depends on the target employers, preferred career path and type of implementation work.

What is the difference between an ERP Consultant and an ERP Developer?

An ERP Consultant primarily focuses on business requirements, processes, configuration, implementation and users. An ERP Developer focuses more heavily on custom development, integrations, programming and technical modifications to the ERP system.

What skills are most important for an ERP Consultant?

Business analysis, ERP configuration, requirement gathering, communication, process mapping, testing, documentation and troubleshooting are important. SQL, APIs, databases and project management become increasingly useful as consultants progress.

Conclusion

ERP consulting provides a versatile career path for professionals who want to work at the intersection of business operations and technology. The role can extend far beyond basic software support, with experienced consultants moving into ERP implementation, business analysis, solution architecture, process improvement, project management and enterprise applications leadership.

An ERP Consultant's earning potential generally increases as practical implementation experience, platform expertise, industry knowledge and client-facing responsibility grow. Professionals who combine ERP knowledge with skills such as business-process analysis, SQL, data management, integrations, reporting and project coordination can position themselves for more specialised and higher-responsibility opportunities.

Add implementation experience, technical fundamentals, communication skills and relevant certification over time. This combination can create a progression from junior ERP support and consulting roles to senior implementation, solution architecture and ERP management positions.
