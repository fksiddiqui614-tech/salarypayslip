Internal Controls Auditors Review

A major part of Internal Auditing involves evaluating whether an organisation's procedures are strong enough to protect assets, maintain reliable records, reduce errors and support compliance. Auditors examine controls across finance as well as operational departments.

💰 Financial Controls

Review payment approvals, reconciliations, accounting entries, cash handling and financial reporting procedures.

📦 Inventory Controls

Evaluate stock records, warehouse procedures, physical verification and movement of inventory.

👥 Payroll Controls

Check employee records, salary changes, attendance data, deductions, approvals and payroll processing.

🛒 Procurement Controls

Examine purchasing approvals, vendor selection, purchase orders and segregation of duties.

💻 System Controls

Assess user access, permissions, system changes and controls surrounding digital financial information.

📋 Compliance Controls

Determine whether internal policies, procedures and applicable regulatory requirements are being followed.

Risk Management Skills for Internal Auditors

Modern Internal Audit functions increasingly focus on business risk rather than simply checking historical transactions. Auditors need to understand where an organisation is most exposed and determine whether existing controls adequately address those risks.

⚠️ Risk Identification

Identify financial, operational, compliance, technology and process risks that could affect organisational objectives.

📊 Risk Assessment

Evaluate the likelihood and potential impact of identified risks to help determine audit priorities.

🛡️ Control Evaluation

Determine whether existing controls are properly designed and operating effectively.

🔎 Fraud Risk

Recognise unusual transactions, weak approval processes and other indicators that may require additional investigation.

🏢 Operational Risk

Review business processes to identify inefficiencies, bottlenecks and weaknesses that can affect performance.

📈 Risk Reporting

Present significant findings clearly so management can prioritise corrective actions and strengthen controls.

Risk-Based Audit Advantage

Risk-based auditing can help professionals move beyond routine checking and develop stronger skills in business analysis, governance and strategic decision-making.

What Does an Internal Auditor Do?

Internal Auditors examine how an organisation operates and determine whether financial, operational and administrative processes are properly controlled. Their work can involve reviewing documents, interviewing employees, analysing transactions and following up on previously identified weaknesses.

Responsibility What It Involves
Audit Planning Understanding business areas, identifying important risks and preparing an appropriate audit scope.
Transaction Testing Checking selected transactions and supporting documents against policies and approval requirements.
Control Testing Determining whether key controls are properly designed and consistently followed.
Process Review Examining operational workflows to identify inefficiencies, gaps and unnecessary exposure.
Audit Documentation Maintaining working papers, evidence and clear records supporting audit conclusions.
Management Reporting Communicating findings, risk observations and recommendations to relevant managers.
Follow-Up Reviews Checking whether departments have implemented agreed corrective actions.
🔎
Daily Work Insight

Internal auditing requires more than finding mistakes. A strong auditor should understand why a weakness occurred, assess its potential business impact and recommend a practical improvement that management can implement.

Internal Audit Reporting and Management Communication

Audit findings only become useful when they are communicated clearly. Internal Auditors prepare reports that explain control weaknesses, supporting evidence, risk implications and the actions management should consider.

Report Element Purpose
Audit Finding Explains the specific weakness or exception identified during the review.
Evidence Provides documentation or analysis supporting the audit observation.
Risk Impact Describes how the weakness could affect financial, operational or compliance objectives.
Root Cause Explores why the problem occurred rather than focusing only on the visible error.
Recommendation Suggests a practical measure for reducing or eliminating the identified risk.
Management Response Records the responsible department's response and planned corrective action.
Follow-Up Status Tracks whether agreed improvements have been completed.

How to Become an Internal Auditor in Pakistan

There is no single route into Internal Auditing. Many professionals begin with accounting, finance or business education and then build practical experience in audit, accounts, controls or compliance. Professional certifications can become increasingly useful as responsibilities grow.

01

Choose an Accounting or Finance Foundation

A B.Com, BS Accounting & Finance, BBA Finance or a related degree can provide the basic knowledge required to understand financial records, business processes and internal controls.

02

Start With a Junior Finance or Audit Role

Entry-level positions such as Internal Audit Assistant, Audit Trainee, Accounts Officer or Junior Auditor can provide useful exposure to documentation and control procedures.

03

Learn Internal Control Testing

Develop the ability to review approvals, reconciliations, segregation of duties, inventory processes, payroll controls and other business procedures.

04

Develop Risk-Based Audit Skills

Learn how to identify important risks, evaluate their impact and design audit procedures around areas that require greater management attention.

05

Add Professional Certification

Qualifications such as CIA, ACCA, CA or CMA can strengthen professional knowledge and may improve access to specialised and senior audit positions.

06

Move Into Senior Audit Responsibilities

With experience, auditors can take responsibility for audit planning, team supervision, management reporting, investigations and enterprise-level risk reviews.

🎯
Career Development Tip

Build your career around three areas at the same time: accounting knowledge, audit judgement and technology skills. This combination can help you move beyond routine checking into higher-value risk and governance work.

Frequently Asked Questions About Internal Auditor Salary in Pakistan

What is the average Internal Auditor salary in Pakistan?

Internal Auditor salaries in Pakistan vary by experience, qualification, employer and city. Junior professionals may earn around PKR 40,000–70,000 per month, while experienced auditors can earn considerably more in large organisations and specialised industries.

What is the starting salary of an Internal Auditor in Pakistan?

Entry-level Internal Auditors and audit trainees may start at approximately PKR 35,000–60,000 per month. The actual starting package depends on the employer, educational background, city and previous accounting or audit experience.

Can an Internal Auditor earn more than PKR 200,000 per month?

Yes. Senior Internal Auditors, Audit Managers and professionals working in banking, multinational companies, specialised risk functions or technology-focused audit roles can move beyond PKR 200,000 per month.

Is Internal Auditing a good career in Pakistan?

Internal Auditing can be a strong career option for people interested in accounting, business controls, risk assessment and problem solving. Experience can also lead into compliance, risk management, governance and senior finance positions.

Which degree is best for becoming an Internal Auditor?

Degrees such as BS Accounting & Finance, B.Com, BBA Finance and related accounting or business programmes can provide a suitable foundation. Professional qualifications can add further value as the career progresses.

Is CIA useful for an Internal Auditor in Pakistan?

The Certified Internal Auditor qualification is specifically focused on Internal Auditing, risk, controls and governance. It can be useful for professionals who want to build a long-term career in internal assurance and audit leadership.

Can ACCA graduates become Internal Auditors?

Yes. ACCA provides knowledge of accounting, financial reporting, audit and related business areas that can be relevant to Internal Audit positions. Practical audit and control experience remains important when applying for jobs.

Do Internal Auditors need accounting experience?

Accounting experience can be highly useful because Internal Auditors frequently examine financial records, reconciliations, transactions and control procedures. However, some organisations recruit graduates directly into audit trainee positions.

What skills are required for an Internal Auditor?

Important skills include financial analysis, internal control testing, risk assessment, Excel, report writing, communication, critical thinking and attention to detail. ERP and data analytics knowledge can provide an additional advantage.

Does Excel matter for Internal Audit jobs?

Yes. Auditors often use Excel for transaction testing, reconciliations, sampling, data analysis and audit schedules. Advanced spreadsheet skills can make it easier to handle larger datasets and prepare supporting analysis.

Is SAP knowledge useful for Internal Auditors?

SAP and other ERP systems can be valuable because many large organisations use automated accounting and operational systems. Understanding how transactions flow through an ERP can improve an auditor's ability to evaluate controls.

Which city has the most Internal Auditor jobs in Pakistan?

Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad generally provide a wider range of Internal Audit opportunities because they have large corporate groups, banks, multinational organisations and professional services firms.

Can an Internal Auditor work remotely?

Some Internal Audit activities can be performed remotely, particularly documentation reviews, data analysis and certain technology-enabled audit procedures. However, assignments involving physical verification, site visits or inventory checks may require on-site work.

What is the difference between an Internal Auditor and an External Auditor?

Internal Auditors work within or for an organisation to evaluate controls, risks and business processes on an ongoing basis. External Auditors provide an independent audit of financial statements according to applicable professional and regulatory requirements.

What can an Internal Auditor become after gaining experience?

Career progression may lead to Senior Internal Auditor, Internal Audit Manager, Head of Internal Audit or Chief Audit Executive roles. Professionals can also move into risk management, compliance, governance, forensic audit and financial control positions.

Conclusion

Internal Auditing offers a practical career path for finance and accounting professionals who enjoy analysing business processes, identifying weaknesses and helping organisations improve their controls. The profession combines financial knowledge with investigation, communication, risk assessment and increasingly important technology skills.

Internal Auditor salaries in Pakistan can range from entry-level compensation to substantially higher packages for professionals who develop strong qualifications, industry experience, leadership ability and specialised knowledge.
