Orthodontist Salary in Pakistan Quick Answer

An Orthodontist in Pakistan typically earns PKR 250,000 to PKR 1,500,000+ per month. A general dentist with a BDS earns roughly PKR 70,000 to 180,000, so completing FCPS Orthodontics is what moves a dentist into the higher band. Most consultants who have finished training earn PKR 400,000 to PKR 1,300,000 a month, and established clinic owners can earn more.

Orthodontist Salary by Qualification

A BDS lets a dentist practise general dentistry. To be recognised as an orthodontist, a dentist normally completes specialist training, most commonly FCPS Orthodontics through the College of Physicians and Surgeons Pakistan (CPSP), or a postgraduate degree from a university. The specialist qualification is the main reason orthodontists earn several times more than general dentists.

How Orthodontists Are Paid in Pakistan

Orthodontic treatment usually lasts 18 to 30 months, so a patient is normally charged a full case fee that is paid in a down payment and monthly instalments, plus visit or adjustment fees. This means an orthodontist's monthly income depends on how many active cases they carry, not only on new patients each month.

Consultants working in private hospitals or another dentist's clinic are commonly paid a share of the treatment fee rather than a fixed salary, or a fixed salary plus a share. Clinic owners earn the treatment fee but also pay for rent, staff, materials, lab work and equipment, so their profit is lower than their billing.

Complex work such as adult treatment, skeletal correction, lingual braces and clear aligner cases carries higher fees than routine fixed braces. Aligner treatment also needs training with the aligner system and digital scanning, which is why orthodontists who add it can raise their income.

How to Become an Orthodontist in Pakistan

  1. Complete FSc Pre-Medical and pass the MDCAT.
  2. Earn a BDS from a PMDC-recognised dental college (5 years).
  3. Complete the one-year house job.
  4. Enter FCPS Orthodontics through CPSP, or a postgraduate orthodontics programme at a university. Training usually takes several years under supervision.
  5. Register as a specialist with PMDC, then work as a consultant in a hospital or open a private clinic.

Admission to specialist training is competitive, and a strong BDS result, house job performance and the entry test all matter.

Unlike software or data roles, a portfolio does not get an orthodontist hired abroad — a licence does. Each country requires its own exams and registration before a Pakistani-trained orthodontist can practise: Gulf countries generally have the fastest route through a licensing exam and a period of supervised or evaluated practice; the UK requires an overseas registration exam through the General Dental Council; the US and Canada require passing their national board exams and, in most cases, redoing part of specialist training, which can take several years and is the slowest and most expensive route of the group. Because of this, most Pakistani orthodontists who work abroad go to the Gulf first, where FCPS is more directly recognised, rather than attempting the US or UK route straight away.

Future Scope of Orthodontics in Pakistan

Demand is rising because more adults and teenagers want alignment treatment, clear aligners have made treatment more acceptable, and private dental clinics are expanding in every major city. Pakistan has relatively few specialists compared with the number of general dentists, so qualified orthodontists have strong prospects in private practice, teaching and research. The main pressures are high equipment costs, competition from general dentists offering aligners, and the long training period.
