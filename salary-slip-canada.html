What is a Canadian Pay Stub?

A Canadian pay stub (salary slip) is an official document provided by employers that shows employee earnings, deductions, and net pay. It includes gross salary, tax deductions, CPP (Canada Pension Plan), EI (Employment Insurance), and final take-home pay.

In Canada, salary structure is regulated through federal and provincial tax systems. Every employee receives deductions automatically before receiving their net salary.

Canada Pension Plan (CPP) Contribution

CPP (Canada Pension Plan) is a mandatory payroll deduction that is applied to every employed individual’s salary in Canada. It is designed to provide financial support after retirement, as well as benefits in cases of disability or death (survivor benefits). In 2026, the CPP contribution rate is approximately 5.95% of an employee’s gross salary. This amount is automatically deducted from the employee’s income each pay period. In addition, the employer contributes an equal percentage, meaning both the employee and employer share the cost of funding the pension system equally.

Employment Insurance (EI) Deduction

Employment Insurance (EI) is a government safety program in Canada that provides financial support to individuals during periods of job loss, maternity leave, parental leave, or illness. Employees contribute approximately 1.66% of their gross salary toward EI. This amount is automatically deducted through the payroll system each pay period. EI helps ensure temporary income support when individuals are unable to work due to eligible life events.

Federal & Provincial Taxes in Canada

In Canada, income tax is applied at two levels: Federal tax and Provincial tax. Both combined determine the total tax deduction from an individual’s salary. Tax rates are based on income brackets and increase progressively as income rises. Typically, tax rates range from 15% to over 33%, depending on the total annual income. Higher-income earners are subject to higher tax rates, while lower-income brackets are taxed at lower rates. This progressive tax system ensures fair contribution based on earning capacity.

Gross Salary to Net Salary Calculation

The final step of a Canadian salary slip is the net salary calculation, where all deductions such as CPP, EI, and income taxes are subtracted from the gross salary. Formula: Gross Salary − (CPP + EI + Taxes) = Net Salary (Take-home Pay) The net salary is the actual amount an employee receives in their bank account after all mandatory deductions have been applied. It represents the final take-home income.

Conclusion: Understanding Your Canadian Pay Stub

Canadian pay stubs are an essential part of understanding your real income. With deductions like CPP, EI, and federal/provincial taxes, your gross salary is reduced to net take-home pay. Knowing this breakdown helps employees better manage finances and plan savings.
