Accountant Roles and Responsibilities in USA

Financial Reporting

Prepare and review financial statements, account balances and supporting documentation used for internal and external reporting.

General Ledger Accounting

Maintain accounting records, reconcile accounts and investigate unusual transactions or discrepancies.

Tax Accounting

Prepare tax-related records, calculate obligations and help organizations meet applicable reporting and filing requirements.

Audit Support

Examine financial information, test controls and organize evidence used during internal or external audit procedures.

Budget and Financial Analysis

Analyze financial results, compare actual performance with budgets and provide information that supports business planning.

Compliance Documentation

Maintain accurate records and help organizations follow accounting, financial reporting and regulatory requirements.

Financial Reporting & Accounting Work

Financial reporting is a central part of many accounting positions. Accountants organize transactions and supporting documentation so that financial statements accurately reflect an organization's activities.

Typical work can include journal entries, reconciliations, balance sheet accounts, income statement preparation, cash-flow analysis and month-end or year-end closing procedures.

As accountants become more experienced, they may review the work of other team members, investigate complex transactions and contribute to financial planning and management decisions.

Audit & Internal Controls

Accountants working in audit examine financial information and supporting records to determine whether transactions and reports are properly documented.

Internal-control work may involve reviewing approval processes, segregation of duties, reconciliations, access controls and procedures designed to reduce financial reporting errors.

Audit experience can also develop skills in risk assessment, evidence review, professional documentation and communication with clients or internal departments.

Tax Accounting & Compliance

Tax-focused accountants work with financial records and tax information to prepare returns, support compliance activities and identify relevant tax issues.

Tax work can become especially demanding during filing periods, when accountants may handle multiple clients, businesses or reporting requirements at the same time.

Experience with federal, state and local tax rules can help accountants develop a specialized career path in taxation.

Accounting Data & Financial Analysis

Modern accountants increasingly work with accounting software, enterprise systems, spreadsheets and financial databases. Instead of only recording transactions, many professionals use financial data to explain business performance.

Common analytical activities include comparing revenue and expenses, reviewing margins, identifying unusual changes, preparing forecasts and creating management reports.

Technology can reduce repetitive data-entry work while giving accountants more time for analysis, review, controls and advisory responsibilities.

How to Become an Accountant in the USA

Becoming an accountant generally begins with a strong foundation in accounting, finance and business principles.

  1. Complete a bachelor's degree. Study accounting or a related business field.
  2. Build accounting fundamentals. Develop knowledge of financial reporting, taxation, auditing and accounting systems.
  3. Gain practical experience. Internships, entry-level accounting positions and supervised work can provide valuable experience.
  4. Choose a specialization. Possible directions include tax, audit, corporate accounting, management accounting, financial reporting or advisory work.
  5. Consider professional certification. Credentials such as the CPA can support certain accounting career paths.
  6. Progress into advanced responsibilities. Experienced accountants may move into senior, supervisory, managerial or specialized positions.

Future of Accounting

Accounting technology is changing how financial information is collected, processed and reviewed. Cloud accounting platforms, automated workflows, data analytics and artificial intelligence can reduce the amount of time spent on repetitive tasks.

As routine processes become more automated, accountants can spend more time on financial interpretation, controls, planning, compliance, investigation and communication with business leaders.

Professionals who combine accounting knowledge with technology, data-analysis and advisory skills may be well positioned for evolving accounting workflows.

International Accountant Careers in the USA

International accounting professionals can pursue U.S. opportunities, but requirements depend on the position, employer and jurisdiction. Foreign education may need academic evaluation, and certain accounting roles can have additional credential or licensing requirements.

Professionals with international accounting experience may find opportunities in corporate accounting, financial reporting, tax support, audit, accounts management and financial operations after meeting the requirements applicable to their role.

International students studying accounting in the United States can also build relevant experience through internships, accounting coursework and professional networking while following applicable work-authorization rules.

Frequently Asked Questions About Accountant Salary in USA

What is the average Accountant salary in the USA?

The national median salary for Accountants and Auditors is $83,680 per year. The mean annual wage is approximately $94,750.

How much does an Accountant make per month in the USA?

A $83,680 annual median salary works out to approximately $6,973 per month before taxes and other deductions.

What is the hourly pay for an Accountant?

The national median hourly wage is $40.23. The mean hourly equivalent is approximately $45.55.

Can Accountants earn more than $83,680 a year?

Yes. The national wage distribution extends well above the median. The highest-paid 10% of Accountants and Auditors earned more than $144,090 per year.

Which states have the highest Accountant salary levels?

Selected May 2023 state benchmarks included mean annual wages of $113,310 in New York, $113,190 in the District of Columbia, $110,700 in New Jersey, $100,560 in California and $99,360 in Massachusetts. These are 2023 state figures rather than the current 2025 national benchmark.

Does experience increase Accountant salary?

Experience can support higher compensation as accountants take on more complex financial reporting, audit, tax, compliance, analytical or management responsibilities. However, individual salary progression depends on the employer, specialty, credentials and location.

What education is needed to become an Accountant?

A bachelor's degree in accounting or a related field is typically required for accountant and auditor positions. Coursework commonly includes financial accounting, managerial accounting, taxation, auditing and business subjects.

Do Accountants need a CPA or advanced training?

Not every accountant position requires a CPA. However, professional certification can be valuable for certain accounting careers and may support advancement into specialized or higher-responsibility positions. Licensing requirements vary by jurisdiction and role.

Can international students and foreign-trained Accountants work in the USA?

Yes, international professionals can pursue accounting careers in the United States, but they must meet applicable education, credential, employer and work-authorization requirements. Some roles may require additional evaluation or professional licensing.

What factors affect Accountant salaries in the USA?

Important factors include experience, state, industry, employer size, accounting specialty, professional credentials, management responsibility, education and the complexity of the work.

The national median salary for Accountants and Auditors is $83,680 per year, equivalent to approximately $6,973 per month or $40.23 per hour. The national mean annual wage is approximately $94,750.
