Audiologist Salary in USA Quick Answer

Audiology is a specialized healthcare profession focused on hearing, balance and related sensory disorders. The latest U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics data reports a $97,740 mean annual wage and a $46.99 mean hourly wage for Audiologists in May 2025. These figures provide a national occupational benchmark, while individual earnings can differ according to employer, location, experience, workload and clinical setting.

Salary Period Audiologist Pay in USA How It Is Determined

How to Read These Numbers

The BLS mean wage and median wage are different statistical measures. The May 2025 OEWS release lists a $97,740 mean annual wage for Audiologists, while the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook reports a $92,120 median annual wage for May 2024. The monthly and weekly amounts above are calculated from the 2025 mean annual figure and do not account for taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, paid leave, bonuses or other employment benefits.

Audiologist Salary by Education & Professional Training

Audiology requires advanced professional education before independent clinical practice. Education does not automatically determine salary, but the training pathway establishes eligibility for professional practice and can support access to different clinical environments and areas of specialization.

🎓 Doctor of Audiology

The Doctor of Audiology, commonly known as the Au.D., is the principal professional degree for contemporary clinical Audiology practice in the United States.

🧪 Clinical Preparation

Audiology education includes supervised clinical experience designed to prepare students for assessment, diagnosis, hearing rehabilitation and patient-centered care.

🔬 Specialized Knowledge

Additional clinical expertise can be developed in areas such as pediatric care, vestibular assessment, cochlear implants, hearing technology and complex diagnostic services.

📚 Continuing Education

Ongoing professional education can help Audiologists maintain credentials, learn emerging technologies and expand their clinical capabilities.

🎓
Training and Earnings Note

Advanced education and specialization can strengthen professional opportunities, but they should not be interpreted as a guaranteed salary increase. Compensation remains dependent on the position, geographic area, employer and responsibilities attached to the role.

How to Become an Audiologist in the USA

Becoming an Audiologist involves a structured education and clinical training pathway. Students typically complete undergraduate preparation before entering an accredited professional Audiology program and then meet the certification and licensing requirements applicable to their intended state of practice.

Stage What It Involves
1. Undergraduate Education Complete the required college-level education and prerequisite coursework for Audiology doctoral programs.
2. Au.D. Program Complete a Doctor of Audiology program combining academic study with supervised clinical education.
3. Clinical Experience Develop practical skills in hearing assessment, diagnostic procedures, rehabilitation and patient care.
4. Certification Meet applicable professional certification requirements and maintain the credentials required for practice.
5. State Licensure Apply for the Audiology license required by the state where professional services will be provided.
6. Career Development Build clinical expertise through employment, continuing education and potential specialization.

Audiology Specialties in the USA

Audiologists can work with different patient groups and clinical problems. Specialization may allow professionals to focus their careers around particular populations, diagnostic services or hearing and balance technologies.

👶 Pediatric Audiology

Pediatric Audiologists evaluate hearing in infants, children and adolescents while supporting early identification and appropriate intervention.

👵 Adult & Geriatric Audiology

This area focuses on hearing assessment and rehabilitation for adults, including age-related hearing changes and communication needs.

🦻 Cochlear Implant Care

Audiologists involved with cochlear implants may assist with assessment, device programming, rehabilitation and long-term patient follow-up.

⚖️ Vestibular Audiology

Vestibular-focused practice involves assessment of balance-related disorders and diagnostic services associated with the inner ear and balance system.

Audiologist Salary by Employer in USA

Where an Audiologist works can influence the structure of compensation, benefits and professional responsibilities. A hospital-based position may look very different from a private clinic role, even when both positions require the same professional degree and license.

🏥 Hospitals & Medical Centers

Hospital Audiologists may work with complex diagnostic cases, rehabilitation services and multidisciplinary medical teams. Compensation can include a salary combined with institutional benefits.

👨‍⚕️ Physician Offices

🏫 Schools & Educational Systems

Audiologists serving educational environments may focus on children, hearing assessment, communication support and coordination with teachers and families.

Technology Transforming Audiology in the USA

Modern Audiology increasingly combines clinical expertise with sophisticated diagnostic and hearing technologies. New tools can change how Audiologists evaluate patients, select devices and monitor treatment outcomes.

📱 Digital Hearing Technology

Modern hearing devices provide increasingly sophisticated options for individualized amplification, connectivity and patient adjustment.

🔎 Advanced Diagnostics

Digital diagnostic systems can support detailed evaluation of hearing function and help clinicians identify patterns that require additional assessment.

🦻 Implantable Hearing Solutions

Cochlear implants and other implantable technologies create specialized clinical pathways involving assessment, programming and long-term rehabilitation.

💻 Teleaudiology

Remote healthcare technologies can expand access to selected Audiology services and may become increasingly useful for follow-up and patient support.

Audiologist Workload and Daily Responsibilities

Audiology work combines patient interaction, diagnostic evaluation, treatment planning, documentation and ongoing communication with other healthcare professionals. The daily schedule depends heavily on the practice environment and patient population.

🦻 Clinical Responsibilities

Audiologists involved in advanced diagnostics, hearing technology, rehabilitation, balance services or specialized patient populations may have different responsibilities from general clinical roles.

Essential Skills for Audiologists in the USA

Successful Audiologists combine clinical knowledge with communication, diagnostic reasoning and patient-centered care. The skills required can change depending on whether a professional works in a hospital, private clinic, school system, rehabilitation setting or specialized hearing center.

🧠 Clinical Reasoning

Audiologists need to interpret diagnostic findings carefully and connect test results with a patient's symptoms, medical history and functional needs.

🗣️ Patient Communication

Clear explanations help patients and families understand hearing conditions, available treatment options and the practical use of hearing technology.

🔬 Diagnostic Accuracy

Precise testing and careful interpretation are important when identifying hearing-related conditions and deciding whether additional medical evaluation is appropriate.

💻 Technology Skills

Familiarity with digital hearing systems, diagnostic equipment and evolving clinical software can support efficient and effective patient care.

🌍 Internationally Educated Audiologists

Audiologists who completed their education abroad may explore U.S. practice after satisfying the academic, credentialing and professional requirements applicable to their background and intended role.

🎓 Credential Evaluation

International qualifications may need to be reviewed to determine whether previous academic and clinical education meets the standards required for professional practice in the United States.
