How the Auditor Salary Benchmark Is Reported

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reports Auditors within the broader "Accountants and Auditors" occupation, SOC 13-2011, rather than publishing a separate national wage category for Auditors. The May 2025 median for this occupational group was $83,680 per year, equivalent to $40.23 per hour.

This benchmark includes a broad range of accounting and auditing roles. Individual Auditor compensation can therefore be higher or lower depending on specialization, credentials, employer, experience and geographic area.

Auditor Roles & Specializations

Auditing includes several career paths. The exact responsibilities of an Auditor depend on whether the professional works inside an organization, for an accounting firm, for government or in a specialized risk and compliance function.

Auditor Type Typical Work
External Auditor Examines financial statements and supporting records for clients.
Internal Auditor Reviews internal controls, processes, risk and operational procedures.
Financial Auditor Tests financial information and evaluates reporting accuracy.
IT Auditor Reviews technology controls, systems, data security and information processes.
Compliance Auditor Evaluates whether organizations follow applicable policies, regulations and standards.
Government Auditor Reviews financial activity, spending, controls and compliance within government organizations.

What Does an Auditor Do?

Auditors examine financial information and supporting documentation to determine whether records are accurate and whether established controls and reporting procedures are functioning properly.

  • Review financial statements and accounting records
  • Test transactions and supporting documentation
  • Evaluate internal controls and financial processes
  • Identify unusual transactions or potential reporting issues
  • Perform reconciliations and analytical procedures
  • Document audit evidence and conclusions
  • Communicate findings with management and other stakeholders
  • Prepare reports and recommendations

Modern auditing also relies heavily on accounting software, spreadsheets, data-analysis systems, enterprise platforms and other digital tools.

Education & Credentials for Auditors

A bachelor's degree in accounting or a related field is typically required for accountants and auditors. Coursework commonly includes financial accounting, auditing, taxation, business law, economics, finance and accounting information systems.

Common Educational Background

  • Bachelor's degree in Accounting
  • Bachelor's degree in Finance
  • Accounting Information Systems
  • Business Administration with accounting coursework
  • Master's degree for advanced or specialized career paths

Professional Credentials

  • Certified Public Accountant (CPA)
  • Certified Internal Auditor (CIA)
  • Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA)
  • Certified Management Accountant (CMA)

Credential requirements differ by career path and jurisdiction. CPA licensure requirements are established by individual state boards, so candidates should verify the current rules applicable to the state where they intend to practice.

Future of Auditing in the USA

Technology is changing how auditing work is performed. Cloud accounting, automation, artificial intelligence, data analytics and digital financial systems can reduce the amount of time spent on repetitive tasks while increasing the importance of analytical and advisory work.

Auditors increasingly need to understand how financial systems generate data, how controls operate within technology environments and how large datasets can be evaluated efficiently.

Areas Increasingly Important for Auditors

  • Audit data analytics
  • Cloud accounting systems
  • Artificial intelligence and automation
  • Cybersecurity and technology controls
  • Risk management
  • Regulatory compliance
  • Continuous auditing and monitoring
  • Digital financial reporting

The broader occupational outlook indicates that technology may automate some routine accounting activities while making analytical and advisory responsibilities more prominent.

Auditor Salary in USA FAQs

1. What is the average Auditor salary in the USA?

The broader Accountants and Auditors occupation had a mean annual wage of $94,750 in May 2025. The median annual wage was $83,680, which is often a more useful measure of typical pay because it represents the midpoint of the wage distribution.

2. What is the median Auditor salary in the USA?

The May 2025 median annual wage for the broader Accountants and Auditors occupation was $83,680. The corresponding median hourly wage was $40.23. Auditor-only compensation can differ depending on the type of auditing work and employer.

3. How much does an Auditor make per hour?

The national median hourly wage for Accountants and Auditors was $40.23 in May 2025. A simple annual equivalent is approximately $83,680 based on 2,080 work hours per year.

4. How much does an Auditor make per month?

A salary of $83,680 per year corresponds to approximately $6,973 per month before taxes and other payroll deductions when divided across 12 months. Actual monthly pay depends on the employer's payroll schedule and total compensation package.

5. What factors affect Auditor salary?

Auditor compensation can be influenced by experience, education, professional certifications, audit specialization, employer, industry, location, job responsibilities and seniority. Internal, external, government, compliance and technology auditing roles can also have different compensation patterns.

6. Do Auditors need a CPA?

A CPA is not universally required for every Auditor position, but CPA licensure can be important for certain accounting and public-audit career paths. Requirements vary by state and role, so candidates should check the rules of the relevant state board and employer.

7. What degree is needed to become an Auditor?

A bachelor's degree in accounting or a related field is typically the entry-level education requirement for accountants and auditors. Coursework in auditing, financial accounting, taxation, finance and accounting information systems can be useful for an Auditor career.

8. Is auditing a good career in the USA?

Auditing provides career paths across public accounting, corporations, government, financial services, healthcare and other industries. The broader Accountants and Auditors occupation is projected to grow 5 percent from 2025 to 2035, with about 115,300 openings projected annually on average.

9. What is the highest salary for an Auditor?

There is no single maximum Auditor salary because compensation varies by role, employer, location and seniority. For the broader Accountants and Auditors occupation, the highest-paid 10 percent earned more than $144,090 annually in May 2025.

10. Are Auditor salaries expected to increase in the future?

Future Auditor pay will depend on labor-market conditions, experience, employer demand, specialization and changes in the accounting and financial-services industries. The broader Accountants and Auditors occupation is projected to grow 5 percent from 2025 to 2035, while technology is expected to shift some routine work toward more analytical and advisory responsibilities.

Conclusion

The national median salary benchmark for the broader Accountants and Auditors occupation is $83,680 per year, or $40.23 per hour, based on May 2025 data. Auditor compensation can vary significantly according to experience, employer, industry, location, specialization and professional credentials.

Auditing offers several career directions, including external audit, internal audit, compliance, financial reporting, technology audit and risk management. A bachelor's degree is typically the starting educational requirement, while professional credentials can support progression into more specialized or senior positions.

As accounting systems become increasingly digital, Auditors are also working with data analytics, automated workflows, cloud platforms and technology controls. These changes are making analytical, risk-focused and advisory capabilities increasingly relevant to the profession.
