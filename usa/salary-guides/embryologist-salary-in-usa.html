Embryologist Salary in USA: Quick Answer

Embryologists work with reproductive cells and embryos in fertility laboratories, supporting assisted reproductive procedures, embryo development, cryopreservation, embryo assessment and reproductive research. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics does not publish a separate national wage category specifically for Embryologists. Its May 2025 data reports a median annual wage of $103,410 and a median hourly wage of $49.72 for Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists. This broader occupation provides the primary federal salary benchmark for research-focused Embryology careers, although individual Embryologist compensation can vary by experience, laboratory setting, location, specialization, employer, education and professional responsibilities.

Embryo Development & Laboratory Analysis

Embryologists play an important role in monitoring embryo development within controlled laboratory environments. Their work involves observing developmental progression, maintaining suitable laboratory conditions and recording relevant findings throughout fertility treatment and reproductive laboratory workflows. Careful observation and consistent laboratory procedures are important because embryo development must be monitored under precisely controlled conditions.

Embryo culture involves maintaining embryos in carefully controlled laboratory environments while following established procedures for culture, observation and handling. Embryologists monitor developmental changes and maintain accurate records throughout the laboratory process. Laboratory documentation is another important part of Embryology practice. Fertility Laboratory & Assisted Reproductive Procedures

Cryopreservation is another important area of fertility laboratory work. Accurate identification, preparation, storage and monitoring are important for maintaining reliable laboratory workflows.

Embryologist Salary Data Sources and Methodology

Embryologist salary information can be difficult to measure using a single official occupational category because the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics does not publish a separate national wage category specifically for Embryologists. For this reason, this guide uses the BLS Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists occupation as the primary federal salary benchmark for research-focused Embryology careers.

According to the BLS May 2025 data, Medical Scientists, Except Epidemiologists had a median annual wage of $103,410 and a median hourly wage of $49.72. The BLS data also shows that the lowest 10 percent earned less than $64,800 annually, while the highest 10 percent earned more than $177,780. These figures describe the broader Medical Scientists occupation and should not be interpreted as a guaranteed Embryologist salary.

The BLS also reports different wage levels across industries where Medical Scientists work. In May 2025, the median annual wage was $125,700 in research and development in the physical, engineering and life sciences, $106,800 in pharmaceutical and medicine manufacturing, $98,620 in hospitals, and $81,910 in medical and diagnostic laboratories. These industry figures provide additional context for scientific laboratory careers but are not Embryologist-specific salary figures.

Monthly and weekly salary figures presented in this guide are calculated from the applicable annual federal benchmark rather than taken from a separate BLS monthly or weekly Embryologist salary table. For example, dividing the $103,410 annual benchmark by twelve produces an approximate monthly equivalent of $8,618. This calculation is provided for convenience and should not be interpreted as an official BLS monthly salary for Embryologists.

Salary differences can also result from experience, education, laboratory setting, geographic location, professional responsibilities, specialization and employer. Because of these differences, the BLS benchmark should be used as a reference point rather than as a guaranteed compensation level for every Embryologist working in the USA.
