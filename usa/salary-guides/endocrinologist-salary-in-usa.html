Endocrinologist Salary in USA Quick Answer

Endocrinologists are Internal Medicine subspecialists who receive additional training in hormonal, metabolic and endocrine disorders. Their clinical work may involve diabetes, thyroid disease, osteoporosis, adrenal disorders, pituitary conditions and other complex problems affecting the body's hormonal systems.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics does not identify Endocrinologists as a separate national wage occupation. Its May 2025 physician wage information uses broader occupational categories, so a federal physician benchmark can provide useful context without representing a specialty-specific Endocrinologist salary. Actual compensation should be evaluated using specialty, employer and practice-level factors as well.

BLS Physician Benchmark
$262,040

The May 2025 BLS wage data reports a mean annual wage of $262,040 for the broader “Physicians, all other” occupational category. It is a general physician reference rather than an Endocrinologist-specific salary figure.

Monthly Equivalent
≈ $21,837

This monthly amount is calculated by dividing the broader annual BLS benchmark by 12. It does not represent a separately reported monthly wage for Endocrinologists.

Endocrinology Fellowship
2–3 Years

Endocrinology fellowship follows Internal Medicine residency, with program length and additional research or specialized training opportunities varying by institution and career pathway.

Career Focus
Hormone & Metabolic Care

Endocrinologists focus on hormonal and metabolic disorders, including diabetes, thyroid disease, adrenal conditions, pituitary disorders and related endocrine health problems.

🩺
BLS Salary Verification Note

The May 2025 figure of $262,040 belongs to the broader “Physicians, all other” occupational classification and is therefore used here only as a federal physician benchmark. Becoming an Endocrinologist requires several stages of medical education and postgraduate clinical training. Physicians first develop broad medical knowledge through medical school and Internal Medicine residency before moving into specialized Endocrinology training. Complete an accredited medical education program and obtain the medical qualification required to enter postgraduate physician training in the United States.

🧬 Endocrinology Fellowship

Enter accredited fellowship training focused on endocrine, hormonal and metabolic disorders, including diabetes and related long-term conditions.

📜 Certification & Licensing

Meet applicable examination, certification and state medical licensing requirements before beginning independent clinical practice.

Diabetes & Metabolic Disorders

Diabetes and metabolic medicine are major areas of Endocrinology practice in the USA. Endocrinologists help patients manage complex blood glucose disorders, metabolic risk factors and hormone-related conditions that can affect long-term health. Their work may include diagnosis, medication management, glucose monitoring and personalized treatment planning.

🩸 Type 1 & Type 2 Diabetes

Endocrinologists may care for patients with Type 1 diabetes, Type 2 diabetes and other forms of diabetes that require specialist management. Treatment can involve insulin therapy, non-insulin medications, glucose monitoring and ongoing adjustment of the care plan.

📊 Glucose Management

Specialist diabetes care may include reviewing blood glucose patterns, continuous glucose monitoring data and treatment responses. Physicians can adjust therapy based on individual needs, lifestyle factors, complications and changes in metabolic health.

⚖️ Obesity & Metabolic Health

Some Endocrinologists focus on obesity and related metabolic disorders. Clinical management may include medical evaluation, nutrition guidance, weight-management medications and monitoring of conditions associated with excess body weight.

🫀 Diabetes-Related Complications

Endocrinologists may help manage patients who have diabetes-related risks affecting cardiovascular health, kidneys, nerves or other organs. Care often requires coordination with primary-care physicians and other specialists.

🩺
Metabolic Care Note

The scope of diabetes and metabolic practice varies by physician and healthcare setting. Some Endocrinologists primarily manage complex diabetes, while others combine metabolic medicine with thyroid, adrenal, pituitary or bone-related endocrine conditions.

Thyroid Disorders

Thyroid disease is another important area of Endocrinology practice in the USA. Endocrinologists evaluate hormone abnormalities and structural changes affecting the thyroid gland, helping patients receive appropriate treatment and long-term monitoring based on their specific condition.

🦋 Hypothyroidism

Endocrinologists may evaluate patients with an underactive thyroid and determine appropriate hormone replacement treatment. Follow-up commonly includes reviewing symptoms, laboratory results and medication response over time.

⚡ Hyperthyroidism

Overactive thyroid conditions can require specialist assessment to identify the underlying cause and select an appropriate treatment approach. Management may involve medication, monitoring and referral for additional treatment when clinically appropriate.

🔍 Thyroid Nodules

Endocrinologists may assess thyroid nodules discovered during physical examination or imaging. Evaluation can include thyroid function testing, ultrasound assessment and, when indicated, additional diagnostic procedures.

🏥 Thyroid Cancer Care

Some Endocrinologists participate in the ongoing management of patients with thyroid cancer. Their role may include hormone management, surveillance and coordination with surgeons, oncologists, radiologists and other specialists.

🩺
Thyroid Practice Note

The thyroid conditions managed by an Endocrinologist can vary according to practice setting and referral patterns. Physicians may treat common thyroid disorders in outpatient clinics or develop additional expertise in complex nodular disease, thyroid cancer and other specialized endocrine conditions.

Adrenal & Pituitary Conditions

Adrenal and pituitary disorders represent some of the more complex areas of Endocrinology. These conditions can affect multiple hormone systems and may require detailed laboratory evaluation, imaging, long-term monitoring and coordination with other medical specialties.

🧠 Pituitary Disorders

Endocrinologists may evaluate pituitary conditions that alter hormone production or regulation. Clinical management can involve hormone testing, imaging studies, medication therapy and continued monitoring of pituitary function.

🔬 Adrenal Disorders

Adrenal-focused care may include conditions involving cortisol, aldosterone and other adrenal hormones. Physicians assess symptoms, laboratory findings and imaging results to identify hormone excess, deficiency or structural abnormalities.

⚖️ Hormone Imbalances

Certain adrenal and pituitary disorders can cause significant changes in metabolism, blood pressure, growth, reproductive function or energy levels. Endocrinologists work to identify the underlying hormonal disturbance and develop an appropriate treatment plan.

🏥 Multidisciplinary Care

Complex pituitary and adrenal cases may involve collaboration with neurosurgeons, radiologists, oncologists, ophthalmologists and other specialists. The Endocrinologist may help coordinate hormonal evaluation and medical management throughout the patient's care.

🧪
Adrenal & Pituitary Care Note

The frequency and complexity of these conditions can differ considerably between Endocrinology practices. Academic medical centers and referral hospitals may manage a larger number of uncommon endocrine disorders, while community practices may focus more heavily on common hormonal and metabolic conditions.

Bone & Mineral Disorders

Bone and mineral medicine is an important part of Endocrinology, particularly for patients with disorders affecting bone strength, calcium balance, phosphate regulation and vitamin D metabolism. Endocrinologists may provide diagnosis, treatment planning and long-term monitoring for complex metabolic bone conditions.

🦴 Osteoporosis

Endocrinologists may evaluate patients with low bone density and increased fracture risk. Care can include bone-density testing, assessment of underlying risk factors, medication selection and strategies designed to support long-term skeletal health.

🧪 Calcium & Phosphate Disorders

Abnormal calcium or phosphate levels can result from several endocrine and metabolic conditions. Specialist evaluation may include laboratory testing, hormone assessment and investigation of the underlying cause before appropriate treatment is selected.

☀️ Vitamin D & Mineral Metabolism

Endocrinologists may manage disorders involving vitamin D and mineral metabolism when they contribute to abnormal calcium levels or weakened bone. Treatment depends on the underlying condition, laboratory findings and the patient's overall clinical needs.

📊 Long-Term Bone Health

Bone health often requires ongoing assessment rather than a single treatment visit. Endocrinologists may monitor bone-density results, treatment response, fracture risk and changes in metabolic factors over an extended period.

🦴
Bone & Mineral Care Note

The scope of bone and mineral practice varies among Endocrinologists. Some physicians focus on osteoporosis and common metabolic bone conditions, while others work in specialized referral centers managing complex calcium, parathyroid, phosphate or other endocrine-related disorders.

Endocrinology Technology & Modern Diagnostic Tools

Technology has become an important part of modern Endocrinology, particularly in diabetes management, hormone assessment and long-term metabolic monitoring. Endocrinologists may use digital tools and laboratory data to better understand individual patient patterns and adjust treatment over time.

📈 Continuous Glucose Monitoring

Continuous glucose monitoring systems can provide ongoing information about blood glucose patterns throughout the day and night. Endocrinologists may review these readings to identify trends, evaluate treatment response and help patients make informed adjustments to diabetes management.

💉 Insulin Pump & Diabetes Technology

Insulin pumps and related diabetes technologies can support more flexible insulin delivery for appropriate patients. Endocrinologists may help determine whether these systems fit a patient's treatment needs and monitor their effectiveness as diabetes management develops.

🧪 Hormone & Metabolic Testing

Endocrine diagnosis often depends on laboratory testing that measures hormones and metabolic markers. Physicians may use targeted blood or urine tests to investigate thyroid, adrenal, pituitary, calcium, glucose and other hormonal conditions.

📱 Digital Patient Monitoring

Digital health platforms can help patients and clinicians track glucose levels, medication patterns, symptoms and other relevant information between appointments. These tools may support more informed follow-up and individualized treatment decisions.

🔬
Endocrinology Technology Note

The availability and use of endocrine technology can differ between healthcare organizations and individual practices. Advanced diabetes devices, laboratory services and digital monitoring platforms may be more common in specialized endocrine centers, while other practices may rely primarily on conventional clinical assessment and laboratory testing.

Advanced Endocrinology Practice

Endocrinology includes several areas of advanced clinical practice beyond routine hormone evaluation. Physicians may develop additional expertise in complex diabetes, pituitary and adrenal disease, reproductive hormone disorders or metabolic bone conditions depending on their training, patient population and practice environment.

💉 Complex Diabetes & Insulin Management

Endocrinologists may manage patients whose diabetes requires more specialized treatment strategies, including intensive insulin therapy, continuous glucose monitoring and detailed adjustment of medications. Complex cases may also require coordination with diabetes educators and other healthcare professionals.

🧠 Pituitary & Adrenal Disorders

Advanced endocrine practice can involve uncommon or difficult-to-diagnose pituitary and adrenal conditions. Evaluation may require specialized hormone testing, imaging interpretation and long-term monitoring, with surgical or other specialist collaboration when appropriate.

🌸 Reproductive & Hormonal Endocrinology

Some Endocrinologists evaluate hormonal disorders affecting reproductive function, including abnormalities involving sex hormones and endocrine regulation. Care may overlap with reproductive medicine, gynecology, urology or other specialties depending on the patient's condition.

🦴 Osteoporosis & Metabolic Bone Disease

Specialized endocrine care may focus on osteoporosis and disorders involving calcium, phosphate, parathyroid function and bone metabolism. Physicians can use laboratory evaluation and bone-density information to guide treatment and monitor long-term skeletal health.

🔬
Advanced Practice Note

The availability of advanced Endocrinology services differs among practices. Academic centers and referral hospitals may manage more complex endocrine conditions, while community-based physicians may concentrate primarily on common diabetes, thyroid and metabolic disorders.

Endocrine Diagnostic Care

Accurate diagnosis is an important part of Endocrinology because many hormonal and metabolic conditions require laboratory testing, imaging or repeated measurements over time. Endocrinologists interpret these findings alongside symptoms, medical history and treatment response to develop an appropriate care plan.

🦋 Thyroid Function & Hormone Testing

Thyroid evaluation can involve laboratory measurements of thyroid hormones and related markers to investigate conditions such as hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism and other thyroid abnormalities. Results are interpreted within the broader clinical picture rather than viewed in isolation.

🩸 Continuous Glucose & A1C Monitoring

Endocrinologists may use A1C results, glucose measurements and data from continuous glucose monitoring systems to assess diabetes control. Reviewing patterns over time can help guide medication adjustments and individualized management strategies.

🦴 Bone Density & Mineral Assessment

Patients with osteoporosis or suspected metabolic bone disorders may undergo bone-density testing and laboratory evaluation of calcium, vitamin D, phosphate and related markers. These findings can help determine treatment needs and monitor changes in skeletal health.

🧪 Dynamic Hormonal Testing & Follow-Up

Certain endocrine disorders cannot be evaluated adequately with a single routine hormone measurement. Specialized stimulation or suppression tests may be used when appropriate, followed by repeat assessment to determine how the condition responds to treatment.

🩺
Diagnostic Care Note

The tests selected by an Endocrinologist depend on the suspected condition, patient history, symptoms and previous results. Specialized investigations may also require collaboration with laboratories, radiology services or other medical specialists.

What does an Endocrinologist do in the USA?

Endocrinologists diagnose and manage disorders involving hormones and metabolism. Their work may include diabetes, thyroid disease, osteoporosis, adrenal disorders, pituitary conditions and other endocrine or metabolic problems.

How do you become an Endocrinologist in the USA?

The typical pathway includes medical school, Internal Medicine residency and subsequent fellowship training in Endocrinology, Diabetes and Metabolism. Physicians must also meet applicable board certification and state medical licensing requirements.

How long is Endocrinology fellowship in the USA?

Endocrinology fellowship generally follows completion of Internal Medicine residency and provides specialized training in hormonal, metabolic and endocrine disorders. Fellowship length and program structure should be verified with individual accredited programs.

How long does it take to become an Endocrinologist in the USA?

Becoming an Endocrinologist requires medical education followed by Internal Medicine residency and specialized fellowship training. The complete timeline depends on the physician's educational pathway and whether additional research or advanced training is pursued.

Is Endocrinology a medical specialty in the USA?

Yes. Endocrinology, Diabetes and Metabolism is an Internal Medicine subspecialty focused on hormone-producing organs, metabolic conditions and related disorders affecting systems throughout the body.

What conditions do Endocrinologists treat in the USA?

Endocrinologists may treat diabetes, thyroid disorders, osteoporosis, adrenal disease, pituitary disorders, calcium abnormalities, metabolic conditions and selected reproductive or hormonal disorders.

Do Endocrinologists treat diabetes in the USA?

Yes. Diabetes management is a major part of Endocrinology practice. Physicians may help patients manage type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes and other complex forms of diabetes through medication, glucose monitoring, lifestyle management and individualized treatment planning.

Can an Endocrinologist prescribe insulin in the USA?

Yes. Endocrinologists can prescribe insulin and other diabetes medications when clinically appropriate. Treatment decisions depend on the patient's condition, glucose patterns, treatment goals, medical history and response to therapy.

Do Endocrinologists treat thyroid problems in the USA?

Yes. Thyroid care is an important part of Endocrinology. Endocrinologists may evaluate hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, thyroid nodules, thyroid hormone abnormalities and other disorders involving the thyroid gland.

Where do Endocrinologists work in the USA?

Endocrinologists may practice in hospitals, outpatient specialty clinics, academic medical centers, multispecialty physician groups, private practices and healthcare organizations. Many positions are primarily outpatient, although some physicians provide hospital consultation.

Can an Endocrinologist work in private practice in the USA?

Yes. Endocrinologists can work in independent practices, physician-owned groups and multispecialty organizations. Compensation may combine a fixed salary with productivity incentives, benefits, partnership opportunities or other contractual arrangements.

What is the difference between an Endocrinologist and a Primary Care Doctor in the USA?

Primary care physicians manage a broad range of common health conditions, while Endocrinologists focus specifically on hormonal and metabolic disorders. Primary care doctors may refer patients to an Endocrinologist when an endocrine condition requires specialized evaluation or management.

Can an international medical graduate become an Endocrinologist in the USA?

International medical graduates may pursue Endocrinology in the United States after satisfying applicable credentialing, examination, residency, fellowship and state licensing requirements. Individual programs may also have separate eligibility and application policies.

Do Endocrinologists need a medical license in every U.S. state?

Medical licensing is handled under state-specific rules. An Endocrinologist who plans to practice in a different state generally needs to meet that jurisdiction's licensing, credentialing and professional practice requirements.

Is Endocrinology a good career in the USA?

Endocrinology can be a strong career choice for physicians interested in diabetes, hormones, metabolic medicine, longitudinal patient relationships and complex chronic disease management. Career suitability depends on individual interests, workload preferences and desired practice setting.
