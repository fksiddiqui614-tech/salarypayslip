What Is GS Locality Pay in USA?

GS locality pay in USA is a geographic salary adjustment added to the standard General Schedule base rate for eligible federal employees. It is designed to account for differences in federal labor-market conditions across different geographic areas.

A federal employee's basic pay can therefore be higher than the standard General Schedule base salary shown in the nationwide pay table. The applicable locality rate depends primarily on the employee's official duty station.

Pay Component What It Represents
GS Base Pay The standard General Schedule salary determined by the employee's grade and step before locality adjustment.
Locality Adjustment A geographic percentage adjustment associated with the employee's applicable locality pay area.
Locality-Adjusted Basic Pay The employee's basic pay after the applicable locality adjustment is included.
Special Salary Rate A separately authorized rate that may apply to certain occupations, locations or federal employee groups.

For example, the standard 2026 GS-12 Step 1 base salary is $85,216 before locality pay. An eligible employee assigned to a locality area can receive a higher basic salary after the applicable locality adjustment is applied.

🗺️
Locality Pay in USA Note

Locality pay is not a separate bonus or one-time payment. It is incorporated into the applicable basic pay rate for eligible General Schedule employees. The exact rate depends on the employee's official duty station and applicable federal pay rules.

How Does GS Locality Pay Work in USA?

GS locality pay in USA works by applying an approved geographic adjustment to the employee's standard General Schedule base rate. The employee's grade and step establish the underlying base salary, while the applicable locality area determines the geographic adjustment.

This means two federal employees with the same GS grade and step can have different basic salaries when their official duty stations fall within different locality pay areas.

Step How GS Locality Pay Works
1. GS Grade The employee's position is assigned a General Schedule grade based on the level and complexity of the position.
2. GS Step The employee's step determines the applicable base salary within that GS grade.
3. Duty Station The employee's official duty station determines the applicable locality pay area in most cases.
4. Locality Adjustment The applicable locality percentage is used to determine the employee's locality-adjusted basic pay.
5. Basic Pay The resulting locality-adjusted amount becomes the employee's applicable basic pay, subject to federal pay rules and limitations.

Why GS Locality Pay Matters in USA

Locality pay can create a significant difference between the standard GS base rate and the salary actually applicable to an employee's duty station. For this reason, a GS salary figure without its locality adjustment may not represent the employee's full basic pay rate.

For example, a GS-15 Step 10 employee has a standard 2026 base salary of $164,301 before locality pay. The applicable locality adjustment can result in a higher basic salary depending on the employee's official duty station.

💰
Locality Adjustment Note

Locality pay is generally based on the employee's official duty station, not simply the employee's home address or state of residence. The applicable OPM locality table should be checked for the specific duty station.

GS Locality Pay Areas in the USA

GS locality pay areas in the USA are geographic areas used to determine the locality adjustment that applies to eligible General Schedule employees. The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) publishes locality pay tables and area definitions for the applicable geographic regions.

The locality system recognizes differences in federal labor-market conditions between major metropolitan areas and other parts of the country. As a result, employees with the same GS grade and step can receive different basic pay depending on the locality area covering their official duty station.

Locality Pay Category How It Affects GS Pay
Major Metropolitan Locality Area Employees whose official duty stations fall within a designated metropolitan locality area receive the applicable geographic adjustment for that area.
Other Defined Locality Areas Certain geographic regions have their own locality pay percentages based on federal locality definitions.
Rest of U.S. Employees whose duty stations are not covered by a separate locality pay area generally fall under the Rest of U.S. locality rate.

For example, the standard 2026 GS-12 Step 1 base salary is $85,216 before locality pay. The final locality-adjusted basic pay depends on the locality area assigned to the employee's official duty station.

🗺️
Locality Area Note

Locality pay areas are defined by federal rules and may not correspond exactly to state boundaries. Always use the current OPM locality definitions to determine the applicable area for a specific duty station.

How Are GS Locality Areas Defined in the USA?

GS locality areas in the USA are established under federal pay rules to reflect geographic differences in labor-market conditions. OPM uses defined locality boundaries to determine which locality pay percentage applies to an employee's official duty station.

A locality area can include a central metropolitan region together with surrounding counties or other geographic areas that meet the applicable federal criteria. The boundaries are therefore not necessarily identical to city or state borders.

Factor Role in Locality Definition
Geographic Location The employee's official duty station is matched to the applicable locality area.
Labor-Market Conditions Locality pay is designed to account for differences in federal and non-federal labor-market conditions across geographic areas.
Metropolitan Boundaries Some locality areas are based on designated metropolitan regions and associated geographic boundaries.
Federal Definitions OPM publishes the official locality area definitions used to administer General Schedule locality pay.
Annual Pay Tables The applicable locality percentage is published as part of the annual federal pay tables.

Rest of U.S. Locality Pay in the USA

The Rest of U.S. locality pay area covers eligible General Schedule employees whose official duty stations are not included in one of the separately defined locality pay areas.

Rest of U.S. does not mean that an employee receives only the nationwide GS base rate. Eligible employees covered by the Rest of U.S. area receive the locality adjustment established for that locality area.

Pay Level What Applies
GS Base Rate The standard General Schedule grade-and-step rate before locality adjustment.
Rest of U.S. Locality Rate The locality adjustment applicable to eligible duty stations covered by the Rest of U.S. locality area.
Final Basic Pay The applicable basic pay after the Rest of U.S. locality adjustment is included, subject to federal pay limitations.

The Rest of U.S. locality rate can therefore make an employee's basic salary higher than the standard nationwide GS base rate. For comparison, the 2026 GS-15 Step 10 standard base rate is $164,301 before any locality adjustment.

🇺🇸
Rest of U.S. Note

Rest of U.S. is itself a locality pay area. It should not be confused with receiving no locality adjustment. The applicable OPM pay table should be used to determine the current Rest of U.S. rate.

Why Does the Official Duty Station Matter for GS Locality Pay in the USA?

The official duty station matters because GS locality pay is generally determined by the geographic location where the federal employee officially performs their work. The employee's home address alone does not normally determine the applicable locality pay area.

This can be especially important for employees who live outside the locality area where their federal position is officially assigned. The locality associated with the duty station is generally the key factor used to identify the applicable geographic adjustment.

Situation Locality Pay Consideration
Same GS Grade and Step, Different Duty Stations Employees can have different basic pay when their duty stations are covered by different locality areas.
Employee Lives Outside Duty-Station Area The employee's official duty station generally remains the key factor for determining locality pay.
Employee Changes Duty Station A change in official duty station can change the locality area and therefore the applicable locality-adjusted basic pay.
Remote or Special Work Arrangements Applicable locality treatment can depend on the employee's official duty station and the federal rules governing the position.

Example of Why Duty Station Matters

Suppose two federal employees are both GS-12 Step 1. The standard 2026 base salary for that grade and step is $85,216. If their official duty stations fall under different locality areas, their locality-adjusted basic pay can differ even though their GS grade and step are identical.

Official Duty Station Rule

How the GS Locality Adjustment Is Applied in USA

The GS locality adjustment is applied to the standard General Schedule salary rate for an employee's grade and step. The resulting locality-adjusted rate becomes the applicable basic pay rate for that locality, subject to federal pay rules and any applicable special salary rate.

Pay Stage How It Works
1. GS Grade The employee's position is assigned a General Schedule grade such as GS-9, GS-12 or GS-15.
2. GS Step The employee's pay position within that grade is identified from Step 1 through Step 10.
3. Base Rate The standard nationwide General Schedule base salary for the applicable grade and step is identified.
4. Locality Area The employee's official duty station determines the applicable locality pay area.
5. Locality-Adjusted Pay The applicable OPM locality salary table provides the resulting basic pay rate for that grade and step.

Example of GS Locality Pay

For example, the 2026 OPM salary table for the Washington–Baltimore–Arlington locality area incorporates a 33.94% locality payment. The applicable salary is determined from the locality table according to the employee's GS grade and step rather than simply adding a generic percentage to take-home pay.

Likewise, the 2026 Rest of U.S. locality table incorporates a 17.06% locality payment. Employees covered by that locality area therefore use the Rest of U.S. salary table when determining their locality-adjusted GS basic pay.

🧮
Locality Adjustment Note

Locality-adjusted basic pay is not the same as take-home pay. Federal taxes, retirement contributions, insurance premiums and other payroll deductions are applied separately and can reduce the amount an employee receives.

Does Locality Pay Affect Take-Home Pay in USA?

Yes. Locality pay can affect take-home pay indirectly because it increases the employee's applicable basic salary. A higher basic salary generally means a higher gross amount from which applicable payroll deductions are calculated.

However, locality pay itself is not the same as take-home pay. Federal employees may have deductions for federal income tax, state or local taxes where applicable, retirement contributions, health insurance, life insurance and other benefits.

Pay Figure What It Represents
GS Base Salary Standard annual General Schedule rate for the employee's grade and step.
Locality-Adjusted Basic Pay Applicable basic salary after the geographic locality adjustment.
Gross Pay Pay before applicable payroll deductions.
Net or Take-Home Pay Amount remaining after applicable taxes, retirement, insurance and other payroll deductions.

Does a Higher Locality Rate Mean Higher Take-Home Pay?

Not necessarily by the same percentage. A higher locality rate increases basic pay, but the employee's actual take-home amount depends on individual tax status, retirement contributions, insurance elections and other payroll deductions.

💰
Take-Home Pay Note

Use locality-adjusted basic pay as the starting salary figure. Do not treat the published GS locality salary as the employee's final bank deposit.

Special Salary Rates in USA

In addition to the standard General Schedule and locality pay system, some federal employees may be covered by special salary rates. These rates are authorized for certain occupations, grades, agencies or locations when federal pay rules provide a higher rate than the otherwise applicable General Schedule rate.

Special salary rates are not a separate pay system that applies to every GS employee. Eligibility depends on the employee's occupation, grade, agency, location and the specific special rate authorization.

Pay Type How It Works
Standard GS Base Pay The nationwide General Schedule rate determined by grade and step before locality adjustments.
Locality-Adjusted Pay The standard GS rate increased by the locality adjustment applicable to the employee's official duty station.
Special Salary Rate A higher authorized rate that may apply to certain federal occupations, grades, agencies or geographic locations.
Retained Rate A rate that may be retained by an eligible employee under applicable federal pay rules when the employee's circumstances qualify.
💼
2026 Special Salary Rate Note

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management publishes individual 2026 special rate tables identifying the occupations, grades and locations covered by each authorization. When applicable, employees may receive the authorized special rate or another higher rate available under federal pay rules.

GS Locality Pay vs Special Salary Rates in USA

Locality pay and special salary rates both affect federal basic pay, but they serve different purposes. Locality pay is primarily a geographic adjustment, while a special salary rate is an authorized higher rate for specific covered occupations, grades, agencies or locations.

Feature Locality Pay Special Salary Rate

Who It Covers Eligible GS employees assigned to the applicable locality area. Employees meeting the requirements of the specific special rate table.
Location Based Yes. The official duty station is important. Often. Some special tables identify specific locations or locality areas.
Occupation Based Generally no. Yes, many special rate tables are limited to particular occupations.
Rate Used Applicable locality-adjusted GS rate. Applicable authorized special rate when it provides the higher applicable rate under the governing rules.

OPM's 2026 General Schedule guidance states that certain GS employees whose standard locality rate is below an applicable special rate may be entitled to the higher special rate. The exact result depends on the applicable special rate authorization and the employee's circumstances.

Pay limitations can become particularly important for higher-level federal employees and for employees whose applicable locality or special salary rate would otherwise produce a rate above an applicable statutory limit.

What Happens to GS Locality Pay When You Change Duty Stations?

A change in an employee's official duty station can affect the locality pay that applies to their federal salary. If the new duty station is covered by a different locality area, the employee's locality-adjusted basic pay may change even when the employee remains at the same GS grade and step.

Situation Potential Salary Effect
Same GS Grade and Step The employee may continue to have the same underlying GS grade and step while the applicable locality adjustment changes.
Move to Higher Locality Area Locality-adjusted basic pay may be higher when the new duty station has a higher applicable locality rate.
Move to Lower Locality Area Locality-adjusted basic pay may be lower when the new duty station has a lower applicable locality rate.
Move to Rest of U.S. The employee may become subject to the Rest of U.S. locality rate if the new duty station falls within that locality category.

The key point is that GS locality pay follows the applicable official duty station rules. A change in where an employee officially works can therefore change the locality component of basic pay without requiring a change in GS grade.

Duty Station Matters

When evaluating GS salary after a relocation, check the current OPM locality pay area for the employee's new official duty station. Do not determine the applicable locality rate solely from the employee's home address.
