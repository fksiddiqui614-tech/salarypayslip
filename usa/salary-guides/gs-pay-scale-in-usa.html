What Do GS Pay Steps Mean?

A GS step represents progression within a particular grade. An employee does not automatically move to a higher GS grade simply by reaching a higher step. For example, moving from GS-9 Step 5 to GS-9 Step 6 is a within-grade step increase, while moving from GS-9 to GS-11 is a promotion to a higher grade.

Does the Federal Agency Affect GS Salary?

The agency itself does not automatically determine how much a GS employee earns. For example, a GS-12 Step 5 employee has a 2026 standard base salary of $86,659 regardless of whether the position is located at one participating federal agency or another. What can differ between agencies is the type of work, available GS grades, career ladders, promotion opportunities, duty locations and whether certain occupations are covered by special salary rates. This means one agency may offer more opportunities for higher-grade positions in a particular career field even though the underlying GS pay table is the same.

How to Get a GS Federal Job in USA

Getting a GS federal job generally involves finding an eligible vacancy, reviewing the qualification requirements, submitting an application through USAJOBS and the hiring agency's application process, and completing the required evaluation and selection steps. The General Schedule covers grades from GS-1 through GS-15, although not every federal position uses the General Schedule.

Step What to Do
1. Search Federal Jobs Search current federal vacancies on USAJOBS and identify positions that match your education, experience, eligibility and career goals.
2. Check the GS Grade Review the grade listed in the vacancy announcement, such as GS-1, GS-5, GS-7, GS-9, GS-12 or GS-15, along with the position's occupational series.
3. Review Qualifications Check the education, specialized experience, eligibility, citizenship or other hiring-path requirements, and any additional conditions listed in the specific vacancy announcement.
4. Prepare Your Application Prepare a federal resume and provide the documents, questionnaire responses and other information requested in the job announcement.
5. Apply Before the Deadline Submit the application through USAJOBS and any required agency application system before the closing date and time.
6. Complete Evaluation The agency reviews applications for eligibility and qualifications. Depending on the position, applicants may also complete assessments, questionnaires, interviews, background investigations or security checks.
7. Receive a Job Offer The selected candidate may first receive a tentative job offer. After the required background investigation and other applicable pre-employment requirements are completed, the agency may issue a final offer and establish a start date.

GS Grade Qualifications in USA

GS grade qualifications determine whether an applicant meets the minimum education and experience requirements for a federal position. The exact requirements depend on the occupational series, position and grade. For many General Schedule positions, applicants may qualify through education, specialized experience, or an appropriate combination of both when permitted by the applicable qualification standard.

GS Grade Common Education Path Experience Path
GS-1 Generally no specific education requirement under the basic General Schedule qualification standard. Generally no experience requirement under the basic standard.
GS-2 High school graduation or equivalent may qualify for positions covered by the applicable standard. The basic standard generally recognizes 3 months of general experience for GS-2 positions.
GS-3 Typically 1 academic year of post-high-school education for applicable positions. Qualifying general experience may also be accepted depending on the occupational standard.
GS-4 Typically 2 academic years of post-high-school education or an applicable equivalent qualification. Qualifying experience may be accepted under the applicable occupational standard.
GS-5 For many applicable professional and administrative positions, a bachelor's degree or 4 academic years of education above high school may qualify. Qualifying experience may substitute for education when the applicable occupational standard permits it.
GS-7 For applicable positions, qualification may be based on a bachelor's degree with Superior Academic Achievement or 1 full year of graduate-level education. Typically 1 year of specialized experience equivalent to at least GS-5 for positions following the applicable two-grade-interval standard.
GS-9 For applicable positions, qualification may be based on a master's degree or 2 full academic years of progressively higher-level graduate education leading to a master's degree. Typically 1 year of specialized experience equivalent to at least GS-7.
GS-11 For applicable positions, qualification may be based on a doctoral degree or 3 full academic years of progressively higher-level graduate education leading to a doctoral degree. Typically 1 year of specialized experience equivalent to at least GS-9.
GS-12 and Above Education alone generally does not qualify at these grades, except for specific research positions and occupational standards that permit qualifying graduate education. Typically requires 1 year of specialized experience equivalent to the next lower grade level under the applicable qualification standard.

Education and Experience Can Sometimes Be Combined

Federal qualification standards may allow an appropriate combination of education and experience when the applicable occupational standard permits it. This means an applicant does not always need to satisfy the entire qualification requirement through education alone or experience alone.

For example, certain positions may allow qualifying education to be combined with qualifying experience to meet the minimum requirement for a particular GS grade. The exact calculation depends on the occupational series and the qualification standard used for the position.

What Is Specialized Experience?

Specialized experience is work experience that equipped the applicant with the knowledge, skills and abilities needed to successfully perform the duties of the position. For many positions above entry level, OPM qualification standards require specialized experience equivalent to the next lower grade level.

For example, a qualifying GS-9 position may require specialized experience equivalent to GS-7, while a GS-12 position may require specialized experience equivalent to at least GS-11. These are qualification examples under applicable standards, not a universal rule for every federal occupation.

GS Qualification Note

Federal jobs in the USA can have different requirements depending on the agency, occupational series, GS grade and individual position. Applicants must meet the minimum qualifications listed in the vacancy announcement, which may include education, specialized experience, training, licensing, security requirements and other job-specific conditions. The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) establishes Government-wide qualification standards for many General Schedule positions, while agencies provide the specific requirements for individual vacancies.

Education and Experience Requirements for GS Jobs

Education and experience requirements depend on the occupational series. For many two-grade-interval administrative and professional positions, a four-year bachelor's degree can provide a pathway to GS-5, while certain positions may allow qualifying graduate education or specialized experience for higher grades such as GS-7 or GS-9. Higher-grade positions often require specialized experience equivalent to the next lower grade level.

For example, OPM's General Schedule standards for many two-grade-interval administrative and management positions list GS-5 with a four-year bachelor's degree, GS-7 with one full year of graduate education or Superior Academic Achievement, and GS-9 with a master's degree, two full years of progressively higher-level graduate education or qualifying specialized experience. These are examples under applicable qualification standards, not universal requirements for every federal occupation.

Federal Career Guide for USA Jobs

A federal career in the USA can provide a structured path for professional growth, increasing responsibility and higher earning potential. Many civilian federal positions use the General Schedule (GS) system, which contains 15 grades from GS-1 through GS-15, with 10 pay steps within each grade. The grade reflects the level of work, responsibility and qualifications required for the position.

Professional Development GS-7 to GS-9 Develop specialized skills and take on more independent responsibilities.

Senior Professional GS-12 to GS-14 Take on advanced technical, analytical, program or management responsibilities.
Senior-Level Career GS-14 to GS-15 Lead major programs, teams or highly complex professional and managerial responsibilities.

How Federal Employees Advance Their Careers

Federal career progression can occur through several paths. An employee may move to a higher-grade position through promotion, compete for another position with greater responsibility or progress through a career ladder when the position provides a structured advancement path.

Employees can also receive within-grade step increases while remaining in the same GS grade. OPM states that each GS grade contains 10 steps, and step increases are subject to acceptable performance and required waiting periods. The standard waiting periods are generally 1 year for Steps 1–3, 2 years for Steps 4–6 and 3 years for Steps 7–9.

GS Career Progression and Salary Growth

Moving to a higher GS grade can create a larger salary increase than simply moving between steps because each grade has its own salary range. For example, the 2026 standard General Schedule reaches $164,301 at GS-15 Step 10 before locality adjustments. Locality pay can increase basic pay depending on the employee's official duty location.

How Long Does It Take to Move Up in the GS System?

There is no single timeline for reaching a particular GS grade. Career progression depends on the employee's occupation, qualifications, performance, position availability and agency structure. Within the same grade, however, the normal progression from Step 1 to Step 10 can take approximately 18 years when an employee remains in the grade and follows the standard waiting periods.

Employees do not have to remain in one GS grade for their entire career. Promotions and movement into higher-level positions can provide a faster path to greater responsibility and higher base pay when the employee meets the qualification requirements.

Step 1 to Step 10 in the GS Pay Scale

Each GS grade contains 10 pay steps. Steps provide progressive increases in pay while an employee remains within the same grade. An employee can therefore earn more over time without necessarily moving to a higher GS grade.

GS Step How It Works Typical Progression
Step 1 Starting step within a GS grade. Initial rate
Step 2 First within-grade increase, subject to applicable requirements. Higher than Step 1
Step 3 Continued within-grade progression, subject to applicable requirements. Higher than Step 2
Step 4 Next stage of within-grade progression. Higher than Step 3
Step 5 Middle portion of the 10-step range. Higher than Step 4
Step 6 Continued salary progression within the grade. Higher than Step 5
Step 7 Advanced within-grade step. Higher than Step 6
Step 8 Later-stage within-grade progression. Higher than Step 7
Step 9 Second-highest standard step within the grade. Higher than Step 8
Step 10 Highest standard step within the GS grade. Highest rate in that grade

Under the standard waiting-period rules, progression from Step 1 through Step 10 can take approximately 18 years if an employee remains in the same grade and receives the applicable within-grade increases. The normal waiting periods are generally 1 year for Steps 1–3, 2 years for Steps 4–6 and 3 years for Steps 7–9. Within-grade increases are also subject to applicable performance and eligibility requirements.

📈
GS Step Note

Moving through GS steps is different from receiving a promotion to a higher GS grade. A within-grade increase raises pay while the employee remains in the same grade, whereas a promotion moves the employee to a higher grade with a different salary range.

GS Base Pay in USA

GS base pay is the standard salary assigned to a particular General Schedule grade and step before geographic locality pay is added. The base table applies across the General Schedule, while the employee's actual basic pay may be higher after the applicable locality adjustment.

Base Pay Component What It Means

Locality Pay Geographic adjustment added to the applicable GS rate for covered locations.
Special Salary Rate A higher rate that may apply to eligible occupations or groups when OPM establishes special rates.

The 2026 standard General Schedule incorporates a 1% General Schedule increase. OPM publishes annual base rates by grade and step before applicable locality adjustments are applied.

💵
Base Pay Note

Base pay is not necessarily the employee's final basic pay. Locality pay and, where applicable, special salary rates can increase the amount paid above the standard GS base rate.

GS Locality Pay in USA

Locality pay is a geographic adjustment added to GS base pay to account for differences in pay levels between federal and non-federal workers in different geographic areas. The applicable rate depends on the employee's official duty station, not simply the state where the employee lives.

Locality Example 2026 Locality Rate Example
Washington-Baltimore-Arlington 33.94% Locality pay is added to the applicable GS base rate.
Los Angeles-Long Beach 36.47% Applicable GS rates are increased by the locality adjustment.
Dallas-Fort Worth 27.26% Applicable GS rates receive the Dallas-Fort Worth locality adjustment.
Rest of U.S. 17.06% Applies to locations included in the Rest of U.S. locality area.

For example, the 2026 Rest of U.S. locality rate is 17.06%, while Washington-Baltimore-Arlington has a 33.94% locality rate and Dallas-Fort Worth has a 27.26% rate.

OPM publishes locality pay tables for designated locality pay areas across the United States. Employees working in foreign areas generally follow separate overseas pay rules rather than the domestic locality pay tables.

🗺️
Locality Pay Note

The same GS grade and step can produce different basic pay amounts in different locality areas. For an exact salary, the employee's GS grade, step and official duty station must be matched to the appropriate OPM locality table.

GS Promotions in USA

A GS promotion moves a federal employee into a position at a higher grade with greater responsibility, difficulty or qualification requirements. Promotion is different from a within-grade step increase because a promotion changes the employee's GS grade, while a step increase moves the employee to a higher step within the same grade.

Career Movement What Changes Example
Within-Grade Increase Employee remains in the same GS grade but moves to a higher pay step. GS-9 Step 3 → GS-9 Step 4
Promotion Employee moves to a higher GS grade with increased responsibility. GS-9 → GS-11
Career-Ladder Promotion Eligible employees may progress through predetermined higher grades when the position is established with a career-ladder advancement path. For example, GS-7 → GS-9 → GS-11 when the position provides those promotion levels.
Competitive Advancement Employee applies and competes for another higher-level federal position. GS-11 → GS-12 or GS-13

How GS Promotions Work

A promotion is generally based on the employee meeting the qualification requirements for the higher-grade position and the availability of an appropriate position or promotion opportunity. Some positions are designed as career ladders, while others require employees to compete for higher-level vacancies.

Moving from one GS grade to another does not simply depend on how long an employee has worked for the federal government. The employee must meet the qualification requirements and any other conditions associated with the higher-grade position.

📈
GS Promotion Note

Federal promotions are not automatic simply because an employee has reached a certain number of years of service. Qualification requirements, position availability, performance, career-ladder provisions and agency rules can all affect advancement.

Within-Grade Step Increases in USA

A within-grade increase, commonly called a WGI, increases an employee's pay while keeping the employee in the same GS grade. Each General Schedule grade has 10 steps, and eligible employees can progress through those steps after meeting the required waiting period and performance conditions.

Step Range Typical Waiting Period Progression
Steps 1 → 2 → 3 Generally 1 year between increases Early within-grade progression
Steps 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 Generally 2 years between increases Middle within-grade progression
Steps 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 10 Generally 3 years between increases Later within-grade progression

Under the standard waiting periods, progressing from Step 1 to Step 10 within the same GS grade can take approximately 18 years when the employee satisfies the applicable requirements. OPM generally specifies 1-year waiting periods for Steps 1–3, 2-year waiting periods for Steps 4–6 and 3-year waiting periods for Steps 7–9.

WGI vs. GS Promotion

Feature Within-Grade Increase Promotion
GS Grade Remains the same Moves to a higher grade
Pay Step Moves to a higher step New grade and applicable step are established
Qualification Must meet applicable WGI requirements Must meet the higher-position requirements
Career Responsibility Generally remains within the same position level Usually involves greater responsibility or a higher-level position
📊
WGI vs. Promotion Note

A within-grade increase does not change an employee's GS grade. A promotion moves the employee to a higher GS grade and is subject to the qualification and promotion requirements applicable to the new position.

Federal Career Ladders in USA

A federal career ladder is a structured progression path that can allow an employee to advance through predetermined GS grades as qualifications, performance and other requirements are met. Career ladders are especially useful for employees entering federal service at a grade below the full performance level.

Career Ladder Possible Progression Example Career Direction
Entry Professional GS-5 → GS-7 → GS-9 Entry-level to independent professional work
Administrative / Professional GS-7 → GS-9 → GS-11 → GS-12 Developing to experienced professional
Advanced Professional GS-9 → GS-11 → GS-12 → GS-13 Professional to advanced responsibility
Senior Career Path GS-11 → GS-12 → GS-13 → GS-14 Experienced to senior professional

How a GS Career Ladder Works

A career-ladder position can identify a target grade progression in the vacancy announcement. Advancement to the next grade is generally subject to meeting the applicable qualification requirements, demonstrating acceptable performance and satisfying agency or position-specific conditions.

For example, a vacancy may identify a position with promotion potential from a lower entry grade to a higher full-performance grade. The exact grades and promotion potential depend on the position and agency.

Not every federal job is a career-ladder position. Employees who are not in a career ladder can pursue advancement by applying for other federal positions or competing for higher-level opportunities when eligible.

🪜
Career Ladder Note

A career ladder does not guarantee automatic promotion on a fixed date. Eligibility, performance, position requirements and agency procedures still apply. The actual promotion potential is determined by the individual vacancy announcement.

Long-Term Federal Career Growth in USA

Long-term federal career growth can come from a combination of within-grade increases, promotions, career-ladder advancement, specialized experience and movement into positions with greater responsibility. Employees can remain within one occupational series or move into related federal career paths as their qualifications develop.

Career Growth Strategy Potential Benefit
Build Specialized Experience Develop experience that qualifies you for more advanced federal positions.
Complete Relevant Education Meet education requirements for occupations where higher-level education is an accepted qualification pathway.
Progress Through a Career Ladder Advance through predefined grades when the position and employee meet the required conditions.
Apply for Higher GS Positions Compete for positions with greater responsibility and higher grade levels.
Develop Leadership Skills Prepare for supervisory, program-management and senior professional roles.
Use Within-Grade Increases Increase pay while remaining in the same GS grade when WGI requirements are met.

GS Career Growth and Long-Term Earnings

Federal employees can increase their compensation through both step progression within a grade and movement into higher GS grades. Locality pay can also affect the final basic pay depending on the employee's official duty station.

For example, an employee could begin at a lower GS grade, gain specialized experience, progress through a career ladder and eventually compete for higher-grade positions. Over a long career, this combination of grade progression and within-grade increases can create substantially greater earning potential than remaining at the same grade throughout an entire career.

What Helps Federal Employees Move to Higher GS Grades?

The most useful long-term strategies include developing occupation-specific expertise, maintaining strong performance, meeting higher-grade qualification standards, gaining leadership experience and monitoring federal vacancies that match the employee's qualifications.

🚀
Long-Term Career Growth Note

Federal career progression varies by occupation and agency. There is no universal timeline for reaching GS-12, GS-13, GS-14 or GS-15. Advancement depends on qualifications, performance, position availability and the requirements of individual federal positions.

GS Pay Scale in USA FAQs

What is the GS Pay Scale in the USA?

The GS Pay Scale is the federal General Schedule compensation system used for many civilian government employees in the United States. It contains 15 grades from GS-1 through GS-15, with 10 pay steps within each grade. Actual basic pay can vary by grade, step, locality area and applicable special salary rates.

How much does a GS employee make?

A GS employee's salary depends on the employee's GS grade, step and locality area. The standard 2026 General Schedule ranges from $22,584 for GS-1 Step 1 to $164,301 for GS-15 Step 10 before locality adjustments. Locality pay and applicable special salary rates can increase the basic pay rate.

What is the highest GS Pay Grade?

The highest standard General Schedule grade is GS-15. Within GS-15, employees can progress through 10 pay steps, with Step 10 representing the highest rate in the standard GS base table before applicable locality adjustments or special salary rates.

What is the difference between GS grade and GS step?

A GS grade represents the level and complexity of a federal position, while a GS step represents an employee's pay position within that grade. Every GS grade contains 10 steps.

How often do GS employees get step increases?

Under standard within-grade increase rules, the waiting period is generally 1 year for Steps 1–3, 2 years for Steps 4–6 and 3 years for Steps 7–9. Applicable performance and other eligibility requirements also apply.

What is locality pay for federal employees?

Locality pay is a geographic adjustment added to applicable GS pay rates to account for differences in local labor markets. The rate depends on the employee's official duty station and designated locality area.

Does California have a different GS Pay Scale?

California does not have one single statewide GS Pay Scale. Federal employees are assigned to applicable locality areas based on their official duty station. Major California federal employment areas can therefore have different locality-adjusted GS salaries.

Does Texas have locality pay for GS employees?

Yes. Eligible GS employees in designated Texas locality areas receive the applicable locality adjustment. The exact rate depends on the employee's official duty station and locality area.

Can a GS employee move to a higher grade?

Yes. Employees can advance to higher GS grades through career-ladder promotions, competitive applications and other authorized federal advancement opportunities when they meet the applicable qualification and position requirements.

How long does it take to reach GS-15?

There is no universal timeline for reaching GS-15. Advancement depends on the employee's occupation, qualifications, performance, career ladder, position availability and opportunities to compete for higher-level federal positions.

What is a special salary rate?

A special salary rate is an alternative federal pay rate established for certain occupations, locations or employee groups when recruitment or retention conditions support compensation above the standard applicable GS rate.

Is GS salary the same as take-home pay?

No. GS salary represents federal pay before employee deductions. Federal employees may have taxes, retirement contributions, health insurance premiums and other payroll deductions, so take-home pay can be lower than the published GS salary.
