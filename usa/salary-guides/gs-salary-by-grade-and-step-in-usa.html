GS Salary Overview: Annual, Monthly, Weekly & Hourly Pay

GS employees are normally paid according to an annual salary rate. Monthly, weekly and hourly equivalents can be calculated from the annual amount, but the actual federal payroll amount depends on the employee's pay schedule, deductions and applicable compensation rules.

Salary Measure How GS Pay Is Represented
Annual GS Salary The official annual salary rate assigned to the employee's grade, step and applicable locality.
Monthly Salary Equivalent Annual salary divided by 12 for an estimated monthly equivalent.
Weekly Salary Equivalent Annual salary divided by 52 for an estimated weekly equivalent.
Hourly Salary Equivalent An estimated hourly equivalent based on the applicable annual salary and standard federal work-year assumptions.
Take-Home Pay The amount remaining after federal taxes, retirement contributions, insurance and other applicable payroll deductions.

What Is the Highest GS Salary Grade in the USA?

The highest standard grade in the General Schedule is GS-15. GS-15 contains 10 pay steps, allowing eligible employees to progress from Step 1 through Step 10 while remaining within the same grade.

GS-15 is the highest grade within the standard General Schedule system, but it is not the highest level of federal compensation across every federal pay system. Senior federal positions may be covered by other pay systems or executive compensation structures.

GS Salary by Step in USA: Step 1 to Step 10

Every standard General Schedule grade contains 10 pay steps. A GS step represents an employee's position within a particular grade and generally increases an employee's basic pay without requiring a move to a different GS grade.

For example, an employee who remains at GS-12 can progress from GS-12 Step 1 toward GS-12 Step 10 as the applicable within-grade requirements are met.

GS Step How the Step Works Salary Progression
Step 1 Starting rate within a GS grade for most employees. Entry point for the grade
Step 2 First within-grade progression after meeting applicable requirements. Higher than Step 1
Step 3 Continued progression within the same GS grade. Higher than Step 2
Step 4 Beginning of the next standard waiting-period group. Higher than Step 3
Step 5 Mid-range step within the GS grade. Higher than Step 4
Step 6 Continued within-grade salary progression. Higher than Step 5
Step 7 Progression into the final standard waiting-period group. Higher than Step 6
Step 8 Advanced step within the same GS grade. Higher than Step 7
Step 9 Near the maximum standard step for the grade. Higher than Step 8
Step 10 Highest standard step within the GS grade. Maximum standard rate for the grade
📈
GS Step Salary Note

Moving from one GS step to another does not change the employee's GS grade. Instead, it increases the pay rate within the existing grade when the employee meets the applicable federal requirements for a within-grade increase.

How Often Do GS Employees Get Step Increases?

Under the standard federal within-grade increase system, employees generally progress through the first three steps more quickly than the later steps. The waiting period becomes longer as an employee moves toward Step 10.

Step Range Typical Waiting Period Progression
Steps 1–3 1 year Annual within-grade progression when applicable requirements are met
Steps 4–6 2 years Longer waiting period between eligible step increases
Steps 7–9 3 years Longest standard waiting period before the next step
Step 10 Maximum standard step No regular step beyond Step 10 within the same grade

Within-grade increases are subject to applicable federal rules, including acceptable performance and required waiting periods. A step increase is different from a promotion because the employee remains within the same GS grade.

⏱️
Step Increase Note

The waiting periods shown represent the standard framework for within-grade increases. Specific circumstances, service credit, performance requirements or other federal rules can affect an individual employee's eligibility.

GS Locality Pay in USA

Locality pay is an additional geographic adjustment applied to eligible General Schedule salaries. It is designed to account for differences in local labor-market conditions and is based primarily on the employee's official duty station.

This means two federal employees with the same GS grade and step can have different basic salaries when they work in different locality areas.

Pay Component What It Means
GS Base Pay Standard salary determined by the employee's GS grade and step.
Locality Adjustment Geographic adjustment based on the applicable locality area.
Locality-Adjusted Basic Pay The resulting basic pay after the applicable locality adjustment is applied.
Special Salary Rate A higher rate that may apply to certain occupations or employee groups under authorized federal pay rules.

Why Locality Matters for GS Salary

Locality pay can make a substantial difference in federal compensation. Employees in high-cost labor markets may receive a larger geographic adjustment than employees covered by the standard nationwide base rate.

🗺️
Official Duty Station Matters

Locality pay is generally determined by the employee's official work location rather than simply the state where the employee lives. Always check the applicable OPM locality table for the current duty station.

Is GS Salary the Same as Take-Home Pay?

No. A published GS salary is generally a gross compensation figure before employee payroll deductions. The amount deposited into a federal employee's account can therefore be considerably lower than the published annual salary.

Pay Figure What It Represents
GS Base Salary Standard General Schedule rate for the employee's grade and step.
Locality-Adjusted Pay Basic pay after the applicable geographic locality adjustment.
Gross Pay Pay before applicable payroll deductions.
Net or Take-Home Pay Amount remaining after applicable taxes, retirement, insurance and other deductions.

Federal employees may have deductions for federal and state taxes, retirement contributions, health insurance and other benefits. As a result, take-home pay depends on the employee's individual circumstances and cannot be determined from the GS grade alone.

GS Promotions and Salary Increases in USA

A GS employee can increase federal compensation in more than one way. Employees may receive within-grade step increases while remaining in the same grade, or they may move to a higher GS grade through a promotion when the position and applicable qualification requirements allow it.

Career Movement What Changes Salary Effect
Step Increase Employee moves to a higher step within the same GS grade. Higher pay within the existing grade.
Career-Ladder Promotion Employee advances to a higher grade within an established career ladder. Can produce a larger salary increase than a normal step increase.
Competitive Promotion Employee applies for and is selected for a higher-level federal position. Salary increases according to the applicable higher-grade rate.
Specialized Advancement Employee qualifies for a position with greater technical or professional responsibility. Compensation depends on the new position, grade, step and locality.

A promotion to a higher GS grade is different from moving to a higher step. The grade generally reflects the level and responsibility of the federal position, while the step represents the employee's pay position within that grade.

Federal Career Ladders and GS Salary Growth

Some federal positions are structured as career-ladder positions, allowing qualified employees to progress through successively higher grades as they gain experience and meet the requirements of the position.

Career Stage Example GS Level Typical Career Development
Entry Level GS-5 / GS-7 Build foundational experience and perform the duties of the position.
Intermediate Level GS-9 / GS-11 Take on greater technical responsibility and develop specialized expertise.
Experienced Level GS-12 / GS-13 Handle more complex assignments and greater independent responsibility.
Advanced Professional Level GS-14 Perform highly complex work, leadership or advanced specialist duties.
Senior GS Level GS-15 Manage highly complex responsibilities, programs or senior professional assignments.

Not every federal occupation follows the same career ladder. The grade progression available to an employee depends on the occupation, position description, agency structure and qualification requirements.

🚀
Career Ladder Note

A career ladder does not guarantee automatic progression to every higher GS grade. Employees must continue to meet the applicable performance, qualification and position requirements.

Long-Term GS Salary and Federal Career Growth

Long-term federal salary growth can come from a combination of within-grade increases, promotions, locality adjustments and changes in job responsibility. Employees who develop specialized skills and qualify for more advanced positions may have opportunities to move into higher GS grades over the course of their careers.

Career Growth Factor Potential Impact on Federal Pay
Within-Grade Increases Increase salary while the employee remains in the same GS grade.
Higher GS Grade Moves the employee into a higher salary range with greater position responsibility.
Locality Adjustment Changes basic pay according to the applicable geographic locality.
Specialized Skills May help employees qualify for more advanced or specialized federal positions.
Leadership Responsibilities Greater responsibility can support advancement into higher-level positions when available.

For example, an employee may begin at a lower GS grade, progress through steps within that grade, qualify for a higher-grade position and eventually reach an advanced GS level. The actual path varies significantly by federal occupation and agency.

📈
Long-Term Salary Insight

GS salary progression is not determined by years of service alone. Position availability, qualifications, performance, agency needs and applicable federal pay rules all influence an employee's advancement opportunities.

FAQs About GS Salary by Grade and Step in USA

What is the GS salary by grade and step in the USA?

GS salary is determined by the employee's General Schedule grade and step. The standard system contains GS-1 through GS-15, with 10 steps within each grade. The applicable locality adjustment can increase the basic salary above the standard base rate.

What is the lowest GS salary in 2026?

The standard 2026 General Schedule begins at $22,584 per year for GS-1 Step 1 before locality adjustments.

What is the highest GS salary?

The highest standard 2026 General Schedule base rate is $164,301 per year for GS-15 Step 10 before locality adjustments.

How many GS grades are there?

The standard General Schedule contains 15 grades, beginning with GS-1 and ending with GS-15.

How many steps are in each GS grade?

Each standard General Schedule grade contains 10 pay steps, from Step 1 through Step 10.

Does GS Step increase salary?

Yes. Moving to a higher step generally increases an employee's basic salary while keeping the employee within the same GS grade, provided the applicable within-grade requirements are met.

How long does it take to move between GS steps?

Under standard within-grade rules, the waiting period is generally 1 year for Steps 1–3, 2 years for Steps 4–6 and 3 years for Steps 7–9, subject to applicable eligibility and performance requirements.

Does locality pay change GS salary?

Yes. Locality pay can increase the basic salary of eligible GS employees according to the locality area associated with their official duty station.

Can two GS employees have different salaries at the same grade and step?

Yes. Two employees at the same GS grade and step can have different locality-adjusted basic pay when their official duty stations are covered by different locality areas.

Is GS-15 the highest GS grade?

Yes. GS-15 is the highest standard grade within the General Schedule. It contains 10 steps, with Step 10 being the highest standard rate within that grade before applicable locality considerations.

Is GS salary the same as take-home pay?

No. Published GS salary represents gross compensation before applicable payroll deductions. Taxes, retirement contributions, insurance and other deductions can reduce the amount an employee actually receives.

Where can I check official GS salary tables?

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) publishes official General Schedule salary tables and locality pay information. Employees should use the latest OPM tables to verify current federal pay rates.

Conclusion

GS salary in the USA is structured around a clear combination of grade, step and locality. The General Schedule includes GS-1 through GS-15, with 10 steps within each grade, allowing federal employees to progress through established pay levels as their careers develop.

Understanding the difference between a GS grade and a GS step is important when comparing federal salaries. A higher grade generally represents a higher-level position, while movement through the steps can increase pay within the same grade. Locality adjustments can further change the final basic salary based on the employee's official duty station.
