How to Become a Nurse in the USA

Becoming a Registered Nurse requires completion of an approved nursing education program followed by the licensing process established for the state where the candidate intends to practice. The exact administrative requirements can vary by state, so applicants should verify current rules with the appropriate state nursing regulatory authority.

1️⃣ Choose an Approved Nursing Program

Students can pursue an approved nursing education pathway such as an Associate Degree in Nursing or Bachelor of Science in Nursing program. Program requirements, clinical hours and admission standards vary by institution.

2️⃣ Complete Nursing Education

Nursing students study subjects such as anatomy, physiology, pharmacology, patient assessment, nursing practice and clinical care. Approved programs also include supervised clinical experience.

3️⃣ Apply for RN Licensure

After completing the required education, candidates apply for licensure through the nursing regulatory authority in the state where they intend to practice. Applicants must satisfy the state's eligibility and documentation requirements.

4️⃣ Pass the NCLEX-RN

Candidates seeking Registered Nurse licensure generally take the NCLEX-RN examination after meeting the applicable eligibility requirements. Successful completion is an important part of the RN licensing process.

5️⃣ Obtain State RN License

Once the required licensing conditions have been satisfied, the state regulatory authority determines whether the candidate can receive an RN license. Renewal and continuing requirements depend on the jurisdiction.

6️⃣ Begin Professional Nursing Practice

Licensed RNs can pursue employment in hospitals, clinics, outpatient centers, long-term care, home healthcare and other healthcare settings. Additional education and certification can support progression into specialized or advanced nursing roles.

RN Licensing & Examination Requirements in the USA

RN licensure is regulated at the state level. Although the NCLEX-RN is the national examination used for Registered Nurse licensure, candidates must also meet the eligibility and application requirements established by the state nursing regulatory authority.

Licensing Step What It Involves
Nursing Education Completion of an approved nursing education program that satisfies the applicable state requirements.
State Board Application Submission of the required RN licensure application and supporting documentation to the relevant state nursing regulatory authority.
NCLEX-RN Eligibility Meeting the applicable requirements to receive authorization to take the NCLEX-RN examination.
NCLEX-RN Examination Successful completion of the National Council Licensure Examination for Registered Nurses.
State RN License Fulfillment of the state's remaining licensing conditions before beginning practice as a Registered Nurse.
License Renewal Maintaining an active license and completing applicable renewal or continuing education requirements established by the state.

Candidates should confirm current requirements directly with the nursing regulatory authority in the state where they intend to practice.

Types of Nurses in the USA

The U.S. nursing workforce includes professionals with different education levels, licenses, clinical responsibilities and areas of practice. Some nurses provide direct bedside care, while others specialize in advanced clinical assessment, anesthesia, midwifery or population-focused care.

👩‍⚕️ Registered Nurses

Registered Nurses provide direct patient care in hospitals, outpatient facilities, long-term care, physician offices, home healthcare and other clinical environments. BLS reports a May 2025 national mean annual wage of $101,420 for Registered Nurses.

🩺 Nurse Practitioners

Nurse Practitioners are advanced practice nurses who can evaluate patients, manage health conditions, provide treatment and perform other advanced clinical functions within applicable state scope-of-practice rules. The May 2025 BLS mean annual wage was $137,300.

🤱 Nurse Midwives

Nurse Midwives provide specialized care related to pregnancy, childbirth, postpartum care and women's reproductive health. BLS reported a May 2025 mean annual wage of $136,980 for this occupation.

💉 Nurse Anesthetists

Nurse Anesthetists provide anesthesia and related perioperative care as advanced practice nurses. BLS reported a May 2025 national mean annual wage of $248,320, making this the highest-paid nursing occupation listed separately in the BLS national wage table.

🏥 Specialty & Clinical Nurses

Registered Nurses can also develop focused careers in areas such as critical care, emergency nursing, oncology, pediatrics, operating rooms, cardiology and other clinical specialties. BLS does not publish a separate national wage category for every RN specialty.

📋 Nurse Leadership Roles

Experienced nurses may move into charge nurse, clinical coordinator, educator, case-management or nursing leadership positions. Compensation depends on the occupation, employer, responsibilities and applicable classification rather than the title alone.

📊
Nursing Role Classification

Not every nursing title represents a separate BLS occupation. For example, ICU Nurse, Emergency Nurse and Operating Room Nurse are commonly used clinical role descriptions within Registered Nursing. Their compensation can therefore differ by employer, location, specialty responsibilities and experience rather than having one universal BLS national salary.

Nurse Roles and Responsibilities in the USA

Registered Nurses play a central role in delivering and coordinating patient care across hospitals, outpatient clinics, long-term care facilities, home healthcare and other medical environments. Their daily responsibilities depend on the patient's condition, nursing specialty, practice setting and applicable clinical protocols.

🩺 Patient Assessment & Clinical Monitoring

Nurses assess patients, collect vital signs, monitor symptoms and observe changes in clinical condition. They communicate significant findings to the appropriate healthcare professionals and help identify when a patient's condition requires additional evaluation or intervention.

💊 Medication Administration & Treatment Support

Nurses administer medications according to authorized treatment plans and clinical procedures. Depending on their role and practice environment, they may also assist with treatments, wound care, intravenous therapy, patient preparation and other aspects of prescribed clinical care.

🤝 Care Coordination & Patient Education

Nurses help coordinate care between patients, physicians, therapists, caregivers and other members of the healthcare team. They also explain treatment instructions, medications, follow-up requirements and self-care practices to patients and families.

📋 Documentation & Healthcare Team Collaboration

Accurate clinical documentation is an important part of nursing practice. Nurses record assessments, interventions, patient responses and other relevant information while communicating with physicians and other healthcare professionals to support continuity and safe delivery of care.

👩‍⚕️
Nursing Responsibilities Vary by Practice Setting

A nurse working in an intensive care unit may manage critically ill patients and continuous monitoring, while an outpatient nurse may focus more heavily on assessments, procedures, education and follow-up care. Emergency, pediatric, oncology, surgical and community nursing roles can also involve different clinical responsibilities.

Essential Nursing Skills for USA Healthcare Careers

Successful nursing practice requires a combination of clinical knowledge, communication ability, technical competence and professional judgment. Nurses work with patients who may have different medical conditions, treatment needs and levels of acuity, making adaptability an important part of the profession.

🩺 Clinical Assessment

Nurses need strong assessment skills to recognize changes in patient condition, collect relevant clinical information and communicate important findings to physicians and other healthcare professionals.

💊 Medication & Treatment Safety

Knowledge of medications, treatment procedures, infection prevention and patient-safety practices helps nurses deliver care according to established clinical protocols and treatment plans.

🧠 Critical Thinking

Nursing often requires prioritizing multiple patient needs, responding to changes in condition and making appropriate decisions within the nurse's professional scope and clinical responsibilities.

🤝 Communication

Clear communication helps nurses explain care instructions, collaborate with healthcare teams, support families and maintain continuity throughout a patient's treatment.

📋 Documentation

Accurate documentation is essential for recording assessments, interventions, patient responses and other information required for safe and coordinated healthcare delivery.

💻 Digital Healthcare Skills

Modern nurses commonly interact with electronic health records, clinical documentation systems, medication technologies and other digital tools used within healthcare organizations.

Nursing Employment Outlook in the USA

Registered Nursing remains one of the largest healthcare occupations in the United States. Employment opportunities are influenced by population health needs, aging demographics, chronic disease management, hospital demand, outpatient care and the continuing need for skilled patient-care professionals.

🏥 Hospital Demand

Hospitals continue to employ large numbers of Registered Nurses across medical-surgical, critical care, emergency, perioperative and specialty departments.

👵 Aging Population

An aging population can increase demand for healthcare services, chronic condition management, rehabilitation, long-term care and other nursing services.

🏠 Expanding Care Settings

Nursing opportunities extend beyond traditional hospitals into outpatient care, home healthcare, community programs, long-term care and other healthcare environments.

📈 Career Mobility

Registered Nurses can develop specialized clinical expertise or pursue additional education leading toward advanced practice, education, management and other professional nursing pathways.

📊
BLS Employment Outlook

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects employment of Registered Nurses to grow over the 2024–2034 period. BLS also expects substantial numbers of openings each year from employment growth and the need to replace workers who leave the occupation. The exact outlook for an individual nurse can depend on location, specialty, experience and healthcare employer.

Future of Nursing in the USA

The nursing profession is continuing to evolve as healthcare delivery becomes more technology-enabled and patient care increasingly extends across hospitals, outpatient services, homes and community settings. Nurses are likely to remain important participants in prevention, treatment, coordination and long-term patient care.

🤖 Healthcare Technology

Electronic health records, remote monitoring, clinical decision-support systems and other digital technologies are changing how nurses document, monitor and coordinate patient care.

🏠 More Community & Home Care

Healthcare systems continue to use outpatient and home-based services for selected patients, creating nursing opportunities outside traditional hospital environments.

🎓 Advanced Nursing Education

Additional education can allow nurses to pursue advanced practice, leadership, teaching, research and specialized clinical career paths.

🧑‍⚕️ Specialized Patient Care

Complex chronic conditions, aging-related healthcare needs and specialized medical treatments can increase the importance of nurses with focused clinical knowledge and experience.

🚀
Long-Term Nursing Career Potential

Nursing offers multiple directions for professional development. A Registered Nurse may remain in direct patient care, develop expertise in a clinical specialty, move into leadership or education, or complete additional preparation for an advanced nursing occupation. Career outcomes depend on education, licensure, professional experience, location and the needs of individual healthcare organizations.

International Nursing Career Pathways in the USA

The United States is a major destination for internationally educated nurses seeking professional opportunities and advanced clinical experience. However, nurses educated outside the country must complete the applicable credential evaluation, examination, licensing and immigration steps before they can practice as Registered Nurses in the USA.

🌍 Internationally Educated Nurses

📚 Education & Credential Review

After obtaining the appropriate U.S. nursing license, qualified nurses may explore positions in hospitals, outpatient facilities, long-term care, home healthcare, specialty departments and other healthcare organizations.

Frequently Asked Questions About Nurse Salaries in the USA

The following answers address common questions about Nurse compensation in the United States, including BLS wage benchmarks, monthly and hourly equivalents, experience, states, nursing specialties, education, licensing and long-term career opportunities.

What is the average Nurse salary in the USA?

According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Registered Nurses had a mean annual wage of $98,430 in May 2025. This federal figure represents the Registered Nurses occupational category and provides an official national wage benchmark. Individual nurses may earn more or less depending on location, experience, specialty, employer, schedule and professional qualifications.

How much do Nurses earn per month in the USA?

Using the BLS May 2025 mean annual Registered Nurse wage of $98,430, the simple annual-to-monthly calculation is approximately $8,203 per month. This is a mathematical equivalent of the annual wage rather than a separately published BLS monthly salary. Actual monthly pay can change with overtime, shift differentials, bonuses and other compensation arrangements.

What is the hourly salary for a Nurse in the USA?

The BLS May 2025 data reports a mean hourly wage of approximately $47.32 for Registered Nurses. The hourly figure is a national occupational benchmark, so an individual nurse's actual hourly rate can differ according to state, employer, experience, specialty and work schedule.

What is the weekly salary for a Nurse in the USA?

Dividing the BLS mean annual Registered Nurse wage of $98,430 by 52 produces an approximate weekly equivalent of $1,893. This calculation is provided for comparison and does not represent a guaranteed weekly paycheck because nurses may work different schedules and receive additional compensation.

Which states pay Nurses the highest salaries?

Registered Nurse wages vary across U.S. states because of differences in healthcare demand, local labor markets, employer types, specialty needs and regional costs. BLS state-level wage information can help compare nursing markets, but a statewide average does not guarantee that every nurse in that state will receive the same compensation.

Do experienced Nurses earn more than newly licensed Nurses?

Experience can improve earning potential as nurses develop stronger clinical skills, specialty expertise, leadership capabilities and familiarity with complex patient-care environments. However, compensation does not increase at an identical rate for every nurse and may also depend on location, specialty, employer and educational qualifications.

Which nursing specialties can offer higher salaries?

Compensation can vary among nursing specialties because of differences in clinical complexity, required expertise, work environment and demand. Specialized roles such as advanced practice nursing, certain critical-care positions and other high-skill nursing occupations may have different compensation structures from general Registered Nurse positions. Salary comparisons should use the appropriate occupational category rather than assuming that every nursing specialty shares the BLS Registered Nurse benchmark.

How does education affect Nurse salary in the USA?

Education can influence career opportunities by qualifying nurses for different roles, certifications and advanced professional responsibilities. An Associate Degree in Nursing and Bachelor of Science in Nursing can both support Registered Nurse pathways when combined with the applicable licensure requirements, while graduate education may open additional advanced practice, education, research or leadership opportunities.

What qualifications are required to become a Registered Nurse in the USA?

Aspiring Registered Nurses generally complete an approved nursing education program, satisfy the requirements of the relevant state nursing regulatory authority and pass the NCLEX-RN when required for licensure. Additional requirements can vary by state, including application, background and credential documentation requirements.

What factors affect Nurse salaries in the USA?

Registered Nurse compensation can be influenced by geographic location, clinical experience, nursing specialty, education, certification, employer, practice setting, shift schedule, overtime, union or non-union employment arrangements and additional responsibilities. Benefits and retirement contributions can also affect the overall value of a nursing position.

Is nursing a good long-term career in the USA?

Nursing can provide a broad healthcare career with opportunities in hospitals, outpatient facilities, home healthcare, long-term care, community health and specialty services. Registered Nurses can also pursue additional education and move toward advanced practice, management, education, research or specialized clinical roles. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics also projects continued employment opportunities for Registered Nurses through the 2024–2034 period.

Conclusion

Nursing offers a broad healthcare career that combines direct patient care, clinical judgment, communication and ongoing professional development. Registered Nurses can work across hospitals, outpatient services, home healthcare, long-term care and specialized clinical environments, making the profession suitable for a wide range of career interests.

Actual nursing compensation can be influenced by state, metropolitan area, clinical specialty, experience, education, certification, employer, practice setting and work schedule. Night and weekend differentials, overtime, benefits and additional responsibilities can also affect the overall value of an employment package.
