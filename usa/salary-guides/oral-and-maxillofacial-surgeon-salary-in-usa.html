How to Become an Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeon in the USA

Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery is one of the most demanding dental specialties in the United States. Becoming an OMS specialist requires years of university education, professional dental training and advanced surgical residency before independently treating patients.

🎓 Step 1: Complete a Bachelor's Degree

Most future surgeons begin with an undergraduate degree while completing science prerequisites required for admission to dental school.

Graduate from an accredited dental school and develop the clinical foundation required before entering Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery residency.

🏥 Step 3: Complete OMS Residency

Finish an accredited Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery residency focused on anesthesia, facial trauma, reconstructive procedures, pathology and advanced oral surgery.

🚀 Step 4: Build Your Surgical Career

Begin practicing within hospitals, private surgical practices, academic institutions or multidisciplinary healthcare organizations while continuing professional development.

Common Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery Procedures

Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons perform a broad range of surgical procedures involving the mouth, jaws and facial structures. The exact mix of procedures varies depending on practice setting, referral patterns, hospital responsibilities and specialist expertise.

🦴 Dental Implant Surgery

Implant placement, bone grafting and related reconstructive procedures help restore missing teeth and improve long-term oral function.

😷 Corrective Jaw Surgery

Orthognathic surgery improves jaw alignment, facial balance, bite function and overall quality of life for appropriately selected patients.

🚑 Facial Trauma Care

Surgeons manage facial fractures, soft tissue injuries and other traumatic conditions requiring specialized surgical treatment.

🧬 Oral Pathology

Evaluation and surgical management of oral cysts, tumors and other pathological conditions form an important part of specialist practice.

💉 Surgical Anesthesia

Many specialists receive advanced anesthesia training that supports safe outpatient and hospital-based surgical procedures.
