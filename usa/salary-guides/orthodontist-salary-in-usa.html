How to Become an Orthodontist in the USA

Orthodontics is one of the most competitive dental specialties in the United States. Becoming an orthodontist requires several years of education, clinical training and specialty residency before independently treating patients.

🎓 Step 1: Complete a Bachelor's Degree

Most future orthodontists first complete an undergraduate degree while fulfilling dental school admission requirements.

🦷 Step 2: Earn a DDS or DMD Degree

Graduate from an accredited dental school and obtain the knowledge and clinical skills required for general dental practice.

😁 Step 3: Complete an Orthodontic Residency

Finish an accredited orthodontic residency program focused on bite correction, facial growth, braces and clear aligner therapy.

🌟 Step 4: Start Professional Practice

Begin practicing in private clinics, group practices, universities or establish an independent orthodontic practice.

🎯
Career Path Note

Successful orthodontists continue improving clinical skills, adopt modern treatment technologies and build strong patient relationships to support long-term professional success.

Dental Schools & Orthodontic Residency

Orthodontists begin their careers by graduating from an accredited dental school before completing an advanced orthodontic residency program. Admission to residency is highly competitive and requires strong academic performance, clinical skills and professional recommendations.

🎓 Dental School

Students complete an accredited Doctor of Dental Surgery (DDS) or Doctor of Dental Medicine (DMD) program to build the clinical foundation required for specialty training.

😁 Orthodontic Residency

Residency programs provide advanced education in dentofacial orthopedics, facial growth, biomechanics, braces, clear aligners and complex bite correction.

🏆 Clinical Training

Residents gain supervised experience treating children, adolescents and adults using both traditional and modern orthodontic techniques.

📚 Continuing Professional Development

Orthodontists continue expanding their expertise through advanced education, professional meetings and emerging digital treatment technologies.

🎓
Education Insight

Completing residency is only the beginning of an orthodontic career. Continuous learning helps specialists remain current with new appliances, treatment planning techniques and digital workflows.

Common Orthodontic Treatments & Services

Modern orthodontists provide far more than traditional braces. Today's practices offer a wide range of treatment options designed to improve bite function, facial balance and smile aesthetics for patients of all ages.

😁 Metal Braces

Traditional fixed braces remain one of the most effective solutions for correcting moderate to complex orthodontic problems.

✨ Clear Aligners

Clear aligner therapy has become increasingly popular among teenagers and adults seeking a discreet orthodontic treatment option.

🦷 Ceramic Braces

Tooth-colored ceramic brackets provide a less noticeable alternative while delivering effective orthodontic treatment.

👶 Early Orthodontic Care

Interceptive treatment during childhood may guide jaw development and reduce the complexity of future orthodontic procedures.

🙂 Adult Orthodontics

An increasing number of adults seek orthodontic treatment to improve oral health, bite alignment and smile appearance.

🛡️ Retention Therapy

Following active treatment, retainers help maintain tooth alignment and support long-term treatment stability.

🦷
Treatment Note

Treatment recommendations vary according to age, bite condition, oral health, treatment objectives and clinical assessment. Orthodontists create individualized treatment plans for every patient.

Digital Orthodontics & Treatment Technology

Technology continues transforming orthodontic care throughout the United States. Digital workflows improve diagnosis, treatment planning and patient communication while increasing efficiency within modern orthodontic practices.

📷 3D Intraoral Scanning

Digital scanners create accurate three-dimensional impressions without the need for many conventional impression materials.

🤖 AI Treatment Planning

Artificial intelligence supports treatment simulations, case analysis and workflow efficiency in within modern orthodontic workflows.

🖨️ 3D Printing

Three-dimensional printing enables the production of orthodontic models, appliances and treatment planning components.

😊 Digital Smile Visualization

Advanced software helps patients visualize expected treatment outcomes before active orthodontic therapy begins.

📱 Remote Monitoring

Some orthodontic practices use secure digital monitoring tools to review treatment progress between office visits.

💻 Practice Innovation

Digital records, cloud-based systems and integrated imaging continue improving efficiency and patient experience.

Technology Insight

Frequently Asked Questions About Orthodontist Salaries in the USA

Find answers to common questions about orthodontist salaries, private practice, residency training, employer types and career opportunities across the United States.

What is the average orthodontist salary in the USA?

Orthodontists are among the highest-paid dental specialists in the United States. Actual compensation varies according to experience, employer, geographic location, patient demand, production incentives and private practice ownership.

How much do orthodontists earn per month in the USA?

Monthly earnings depend on annual compensation, patient volume, employer, production bonuses and practice performance. Established orthodontists generally earn more than newly qualified specialists.

Which states pay orthodontists the highest salaries?

Orthodontist salaries vary across the United States because of regional demand, cost of living, local competition, population growth and private healthcare markets.

Do orthodontists earn more than general dentists in the USA?

In many cases, yes. Orthodontists complete additional specialty residency training and provide advanced orthodontic treatments, which often results in higher earning potential than general dentistry.

Do orthodontists earn more than oral surgeons?

Both specialties are among the highest-paid fields in dentistry. Actual earnings depend on specialty focus, employer, patient volume, private practice performance and geographic location.

What factors affect orthodontist salaries in the USA?

Professional experience, residency training, employer type, geographic location, patient demand, treatment complexity, production incentives and practice ownership all influence orthodontist compensation.

Do private practice orthodontists make more money?

Private practice ownership may provide greater long-term earning potential because income is influenced by patient volume, business growth, treatment services and overall practice performance.

Can clear aligner treatment increase an orthodontist's income?

Many orthodontic practices offer clear aligner therapy alongside traditional braces. Expanding treatment options may contribute to practice growth and increased overall revenue.

Do experienced orthodontists earn more than new specialists?

Yes. As orthodontists gain clinical experience, strengthen referral networks and build successful practices, their earning potential generally increases over time.

Are orthodontist salaries different in hospitals and private clinics?

Yes. Compensation structures vary between hospitals, universities, dental service organizations and private practices because each employer offers different salary models, benefits and incentive programs.

Can orthodontists increase their income without owning a practice?

Yes. Orthodontists employed by private groups, DSOs or specialty clinics may increase earnings through production bonuses, expanded treatment services, advanced certifications and consistent patient demand without becoming practice owners.

Is orthodontics one of the highest-paying dental specialties in the USA?

Yes. Orthodontics is widely recognized as one of the highest-paying dental specialties because of advanced residency training, specialist expertise and consistent patient demand.

How long does it take to become an orthodontist in the USA?

Future orthodontists generally complete a bachelor's degree, dental school and an accredited orthodontic residency program before becoming licensed specialists. The complete pathway usually requires more than ten years.

What qualifications are required to become an orthodontist in the USA?

Orthodontists typically earn a DDS or DMD degree, complete an accredited orthodontic residency, obtain state licensure and may pursue board certification for additional professional recognition.

Is orthodontics a good long-term career in the USA?

Orthodontics offers strong career stability, excellent earning potential and long-term opportunities in private practice, academic institutions, dental organizations and specialty healthcare settings.
