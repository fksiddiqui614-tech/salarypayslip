Orthopedic Surgery is one of the highest-paid physician specialties in the United States. The same BLS release reports a mean hourly wage of $179.60. Orthopedic Surgeon Education, Residency & Training Path

Orthopedic Surgery requires an extensive sequence of medical education and hands-on postgraduate training. Physicians first complete medical school and then enter orthopedic surgery residency, where they develop experience in musculoskeletal diagnosis, operative procedures, trauma care and postoperative management. Some physicians continue with fellowship training to concentrate on a particular orthopedic subspecialty.

Education or Training Stage What It Provides

Medical School Provides physician-level education in medical science, diagnosis, patient care and clinical decision-making leading to an MD or DO degree

Orthopedic Fellowship Allows additional focused training in areas such as spine, sports medicine, adult reconstruction, hand surgery or orthopedic trauma
Licensed Practice Allows qualified physicians to provide independent orthopedic care after completing the applicable examination, certification and state licensing requirements

How to Become an Orthopedic Surgeon in the USA

Becoming an Orthopedic Surgeon requires a long sequence of academic, clinical and surgical training. After completing an undergraduate education, students attend medical school and then compete for an orthopedic surgery residency. Physicians may later complete fellowship training to develop advanced expertise in areas such as sports medicine, spine surgery, trauma or joint reconstruction.

🎓 Undergraduate Education

Students generally complete a bachelor's degree or an equivalent pre-medical education before applying to medical school. Strong preparation in biological sciences, chemistry and related subjects can support the medical education pathway.

🩺 Medical School

Medical school provides foundational education in anatomy, physiology, diagnosis and patient care before students move into clinical rotations and begin selecting a medical specialty.

🏥 Orthopedic Residency

Orthopedic surgery residency develops practical experience in evaluating musculoskeletal conditions, performing procedures and managing patients before and after surgery.

🏆 Fellowship Training

Additional fellowship training can provide focused experience in areas such as adult reconstruction, sports medicine, hand surgery, spine surgery, trauma and pediatric orthopedics.

🦴
Training Pathway Note

The length and structure of an Orthopedic Surgeon's education can vary according to specialty interests and career plans. Fellowship training is not required for every orthopedic career, but it can provide advanced exposure to specific patient populations and procedures.

Orthopedic Surgery Specialties and Earning Potential

Orthopedic Surgery covers a broad range of clinical problems, from sports injuries and joint degeneration to complex trauma and spinal disorders. Physicians who develop expertise in a particular area may build their careers around a narrower group of procedures and patient needs.

🏃 Sports Medicine

Sports-focused orthopedic specialists treat athletic injuries and other musculoskeletal conditions involving joints, tendons, ligaments and bones. Their work may include both surgical and non-surgical treatment.

🦿 Joint Replacement

Adult reconstruction specialists concentrate on damaged hips, knees and other joints, including surgical replacement and complex revision procedures.

🧠 Spine Surgery

Orthopedic spine specialists manage selected disorders affecting the spine and may perform complex procedures involving the vertebrae, discs and supporting structures.

✋ Hand & Upper Extremity

This area focuses on injuries and disorders affecting the hand, wrist, elbow and surrounding structures, combining detailed anatomy knowledge with specialized surgical techniques.

💰
Specialty Compensation Note

A particular orthopedic subspecialty does not automatically determine an individual's income. Earnings can also be influenced by case complexity, patient demand, geographic market, operating volume, employer incentives, professional experience and ownership arrangements.

🤖 Robotic-Assisted Procedures

Robotic systems can assist with planning and execution during selected orthopedic operations, particularly certain joint replacement procedures.

🖥️ Digital Surgical Planning

Three-dimensional imaging and computer-based planning can help surgeons evaluate anatomy and prepare for complex reconstructive procedures.

What Does an Orthopedic Surgeon Do?

An Orthopedic Surgeon diagnoses and treats conditions affecting the musculoskeletal system, including bones, joints, muscles, ligaments, tendons and related structures. Treatment can range from non-surgical care and rehabilitation planning to complex surgical procedures. The exact role depends on the physician's subspecialty, clinical setting and patient needs.

🩺 Patient Evaluation

Orthopedic Surgeons review symptoms, medical history, physical findings and diagnostic imaging to identify injuries or conditions affecting the musculoskeletal system.

🦴 Treatment Planning

The physician determines whether a patient may benefit from medication, physical therapy, injections, observation or an operative approach based on the diagnosis and individual circumstances.

🔧 Surgical Treatment

When surgery is appropriate, Orthopedic Surgeons perform procedures such as fracture repair, joint replacement, ligament reconstruction and other operations designed to restore function.

👨‍⚕️ Recovery Management

After treatment, surgeons monitor healing, evaluate functional progress, manage complications and coordinate rehabilitation or additional care when necessary.

How Does the Orthopedic Surgery Process Work?

Orthopedic treatment usually follows a structured clinical process rather than moving directly from diagnosis to surgery. The surgeon first determines the underlying problem, discusses available treatment options and evaluates whether an operation is appropriate. If surgery is selected, preparation, the procedure and postoperative recovery are managed as connected stages of care.

Treatment Stage What the Orthopedic Surgeon Does
1. Consultation Reviews the patient's symptoms, medical history, physical condition and specific functional concerns
2. Diagnosis & Imaging Uses examinations and appropriate imaging or diagnostic information to identify the underlying orthopedic problem
3. Treatment Decision Compares conservative and surgical options while considering expected benefits, risks, recovery requirements and patient goals
4. Surgical Preparation Plans the procedure, reviews medical considerations and coordinates with anesthesia, nursing and other members of the healthcare team
5. Operation Performs the selected orthopedic procedure using appropriate surgical techniques and technology
6. Recovery & Follow-Up Monitors healing, evaluates function and works with rehabilitation professionals to support the patient's recovery
🦿
Orthopedic Care Process

Not every orthopedic patient requires surgery. A major part of the Orthopedic Surgeon's role is determining when an operation is appropriate and when non-operative treatment may provide a suitable alternative. The final approach depends on the diagnosis, severity of the condition, patient health and expected treatment outcomes.
