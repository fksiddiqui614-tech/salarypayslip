Periodontists are dental specialists who focus on the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of diseases affecting the gums and supporting structures around the teeth. Periodontist Salary in USA Quick Answer

Periodontics is an advanced dental specialty focused on the health of the gums, periodontal tissues and structures that support the teeth. Periodontists may perform complex periodontal treatment, surgical procedures and implant-related care. Their compensation can vary according to clinical experience, procedure volume, employer, geographic market, referral network, productivity arrangements and practice ownership.

BLS Specialist Benchmark
$225,770

The latest BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook reports a median annual wage of $225,770 for the broader “Dentists, All Other Specialists” category in May 2024. This category is broader than Periodontics and is not a Periodontist-specific salary figure.

Monthly Benchmark
≈ $18,814

This is a simple annual-to-monthly calculation based on the BLS specialist-dentist median and should not be interpreted as guaranteed monthly Periodontist earnings.

Advanced Training

Periodontics requires advanced specialty education after dental school, with accredited programs commonly providing approximately three years of focused postgraduate training.

Career Focus
Gum & Implant Care

Periodontists specialize in periodontal disease management, periodontal surgery, soft-tissue treatment and selected implant-related procedures.

BLS Salary Verification Note

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics does not publish a separate national wage category specifically for Periodontists. In its May 2024 data, the BLS reports a median annual wage of $225,770 for “Dentists, All Other Specialists,” a broader occupational category that includes dental specialists not separately classified by BLS. Therefore, $225,770 should be used as an official specialist-dentist benchmark rather than as an exact national Periodontist salary. Actual Periodontist compensation can differ according to experience, state, referral volume, procedures, employer, productivity and practice ownership.

Frequently Asked Questions About Periodontist Salaries in the USA

Get answers to common questions about Periodontist salary in the USA, specialty education, periodontal procedures, practice ownership, employment settings, geographic pay differences and long-term dental career opportunities.

What is the average Periodontist salary in the USA?

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics does not report Periodontists as a separate national wage occupation. Its broader “Dentists, All Other Specialists” category can provide an official reference point, while actual Periodontist salary in the USA varies by experience, location, practice type, services and compensation structure.

How much does a Periodontist make per year in the USA?

Annual earnings can differ significantly between employed specialists, partners and practice owners. Compensation may include base salary, production incentives, bonuses or income associated with ownership, so a single national figure cannot accurately describe every Periodontist's earnings.

How much does a Periodontist make per month in the USA?

A monthly equivalent can be estimated by dividing annual compensation by twelve, but actual take-home or payroll amounts may vary. Bonuses, production payments, benefits, taxes and ownership income can all affect the amount received during a particular month.

Is Periodontics a high-paying dental specialty in the USA?

Periodontics can offer strong earning potential because specialists complete additional postgraduate training and perform advanced periodontal and surgical procedures. However, compensation differs between markets and should be evaluated using the complete employment or practice arrangement.

How many years does it take to become a Periodontist in the USA?

After completing dental school, dentists generally undertake an additional period of advanced Periodontics education. Accredited specialty programs commonly involve about three years of postgraduate training focused on periodontal diagnosis, surgery and related treatment.

Does Periodontist salary increase with experience?

Experience can influence compensation as a Periodontist develops clinical expertise, referral relationships, patient volume and professional responsibilities. The effect is not automatic because salary also depends on geography, employer, productivity and practice ownership.

Do Periodontists make more in private practice?

Private practice can create higher income potential through production, partnership and ownership, but it also introduces operating expenses and business responsibilities. An employed position may provide a different balance of predictable compensation, benefits and professional risk.

Can a Periodontist earn money from practice ownership?

Yes. A Periodontist who owns or holds an equity interest in a dental practice may receive income beyond clinical compensation. The financial outcome depends on practice revenue, expenses, ownership percentage, debt, staffing and other business factors.

Which states pay Periodontists the most in the USA?

There is no single reliable BLS state ranking specifically for Periodontists because the occupation is not reported separately at that level. State-level opportunities can nevertheless differ because of specialist demand, dentist referral networks, population, competition and local practice economics.

Do Periodontists work with general dentists?

Yes. General dentists frequently refer patients to Periodontists when advanced periodontal evaluation or treatment is needed. Strong professional relationships with referring dentists can therefore be an important part of a Periodontist's clinical practice.

Can Periodontists perform dental implant procedures?

Many Periodontists receive training related to implant placement and the management of gum and bone tissues surrounding implants. The exact services offered depend on the specialist's training, professional scope and individual practice.

What types of patients do Periodontists treat?

Periodontists may treat patients with gum disease, periodontal tissue damage, gum recession and other conditions affecting the supporting structures of teeth. Some also provide specialized implant-related and regenerative procedures.

Can an international dentist become a Periodontist in the USA?

Internationally educated dentists may pursue Periodontics in the USA, but requirements can include credential evaluation, examinations, additional dental education, specialty program admission and state licensing. The appropriate pathway depends on the dentist's qualifications and intended state of practice.

What is the difference between a Periodontist and an Endodontist?

Periodontists focus primarily on the gums and supporting structures around teeth, including periodontal and certain implant-related conditions. Endodontists specialize in the dental pulp and tissues inside and around tooth roots, including complex root canal treatment.

What should I consider besides Periodontist salary in the USA?

Dentists should compare specialty training requirements, clinical responsibilities, patient mix, referral opportunities, benefits, scheduling, malpractice coverage, continuing education, partnership potential and practice culture in addition to advertised compensation.

Conclusion

Choosing a Periodontist career in the USA means developing expertise in a focused area of dental medicine where diagnosis, prevention and advanced treatment of periodontal conditions come together. The earning potential can be attractive, but income is shaped by the type of practice, clinical workload, location, professional experience and the services a Periodontist provides.

The BLS is useful for establishing a government-based reference point for specialist dentists, although its occupational data does not identify Periodontists as a separate national salary category. As a result, BLS figures should be viewed as a benchmark rather than as a definitive representation of what every Periodontist earns in the USA.

A broader career evaluation should also consider the investment required for specialty education, the nature of periodontal procedures, referral relationships, patient demand, workplace flexibility, benefits and the possibility of building or joining a specialty practice. These factors can have a meaningful effect on both professional satisfaction and long-term financial outcomes.
